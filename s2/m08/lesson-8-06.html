📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar — today you pull it back out, and connect it to the past tense you already own.
This is your fourth meeting with the module's grammar and the first day of Week 2. You'll do an ear-quiz on the music words, a short production task, and a spiral that puts two time-frames side by side: the preterite from Módulos 6–7 (to recount a fiesta that already happened) and the present progressive from this module (to describe one happening right now). Telling then from now is exactly what makes you sound fluent.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize spoken music and fiesta words and match them to meaning.
2
I will be able to choose between the preterite (a past fiesta) and the present progressive (one happening now).

You have two tools for two time-frames. Use the preterite (Módulos 6–7) for what already happened, and the present progressive (this module) for what's happening right now. Listen and read across:

Hear both frames collide in one thought: Anoche fui a un concierto, y ahora estoy escuchando la misma canción en casa. — the preterite (what happened) and the progressive (what's happening now), side by side.

✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 7 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check your verb and spelling.

1. Say: “Last weekend I went to a festival.” (preterite of ir)

El fin de semana pasado fui a un festival. A completed past action → preterite; “I went” = fui.

2. Say: “Right now the band is playing a song.”

Ahora la banda está tocando una canción. Happening now → present progressive (está + tocando).

3. Say: “This festival is livelier than last year's.”

Este festival es más alegre que el del año pasado. Comparing → más + adjective + que.
🎉 CULTURA · LAS FIESTAS PATRIAS
Cuando un país entero celebra

Muchos países hispanos celebran sus fiestas patrias (national holidays) con música y baile. En México, el 15 y 16 de septiembre, la gente está cantando y bailando en las plazas. Este año alguien está celebrando ahora mismo; el año pasado celebró igual — antes y ahora, la misma alegría.

🎷 CULTURA A FONDO · LA MÚSICA ANDINA

High in the Andes of Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador, festivals ring with a sound older than the Spanish language itself: the breathy quena (a reed flute) and the zampóña (panpipes), instruments the Inca and their ancestors played centuries ago. At a fiesta today you might hear a musician tocando the quena while a crowd is bailando a huayno. It's a living link between antes and ahora — and a reminder that in the Spanish-speaking world, music carries history in every beat.
