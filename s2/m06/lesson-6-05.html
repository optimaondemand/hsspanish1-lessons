★ A leer — Un viaje a Madrid
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just reading. You'll hear the preterite doing its job inside a real little story.
This is your third encounter with the regular preterite — and the most relaxed. Instead of conjugating in a drill, you'll simply notice past-tense verbs at work inside meaningful input: a short first-person account of a trip to Madrid, this module's featured city. Read it, listen to it, and let the pattern settle. Then we take a culture deep-dive into España and its great museum, el Prado.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and understand a short past-tense narrative and identify the preterite verbs in it.
2
I will be able to locate Madrid and name one thing el Prado is famous for.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 8 min

Before you read

Good readers guess first. You're about to read someone's account of a trip to Madrid last week. As you go, keep one question in mind: which verbs are in the past? Look for the endings you learned — -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron and -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -ieron.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
If you traveled somewhere new, what would you most want to tell people afterward?
📚 PART 2 · LA LECTURA · MADRID 16 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know the important ones.

Escuchar la lectura completa — Un viaje a Madrid

«Hola. La semana pasada viajé a España con mi familia. Llegamos a Madrid, la capital, y pasamos tres días allí. El primer día caminamos por el centro y visitamos el Museo del Prado. Miré cuadros de Velázquez y de Goya. Por la noche cené en un restaurante y probé una tortilla española. El sábado mis padres compraron recuerdos y yo tomé muchas fotos. Terminamos el viaje muy contentos. ¡Me encantó Madrid!»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

llegamos = we arrived · pasamos tres días = we spent three days · el primer día = the first day · por el centro = around downtown · los cuadros = the paintings · cené = I had dinner · probé = I tried (tasted) · una tortilla española = a Spanish potato omelette · los recuerdos = souvenirs · tomé fotos = I took photos · me encantó = I loved it
✅ PART 3 · ¿COMPRENDISTE? 14 min

Check your understanding

Answer from the paragraph — these are reading questions.

✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Adónde viajó la persona la semana pasada?
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Qué museo visitaron?
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Qué probó la persona en el restaurante?

Be a detective: find three preterite verbs in the paragraph and name each ending. Say them out loud, then reveal.

Examples: viajé (yo, -é), visitamos (nosotros, -amos), compraron (ellos, -aron), probé (yo, -é), encantó (-ó). Every one is stem + a past ending you know.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Rewrite one sentence of the paragraph as if you did it: change «Viajé a España» to a real place you actually went (Viajé a…). Then read your version aloud.
🌎 CULTURA · MADRID Y EL PRADO
La capital y su gran museo

Madrid es la capital de España y está en el centro del país (a perfect ser/estar pair from Semester 1). Right in the city sits the Museo del Prado, one of the most important art museums in the world, opened in 1819.

Its walls hold masterpieces by Diego Velázquez (Las Meninas) and Francisco de Goya — painters whose work you can still stand in front of today. When our narrator says «miré cuadros de Velázquez y de Goya,» that's a real thing millions of visitors do every year.

🌎 ESPAÑA A FONDO
Más allá de Madrid

España is a country of striking variety. Beyond Madrid you'll find Barcelona and Gaudí's dreamlike architecture on the Mediterranean, the Alhambra palace in Granada (built when much of Spain was ruled by Muslim kingdoms), the pilgrim road of the Camino de Santiago in the green north, and the flamenco of Andalucía in the south.

Food is regional too: la tortilla española (a potato omelette), el gazpacho (a cold tomato soup), and las tapas (small shared plates). One reminder for this course: we keep our everyday register Latin-American — in Spain you'd hear vosotros for “you all,” but we use ustedes.
