📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned when. Today you meet the verbs — and the endings that move them into the past.
First a set of everyday regular verbs (viajar, visitar, caminar…). Then the big idea of the module: the regular preterite, Spanish's main past tense. Today you meet the endings by themselves — the -ar set and the -er/-ir set — and just listen to real examples. We are not drilling the full contrast yet; tomorrow (Lesson 6.03) is the careful, full lesson. One thing at a time, because this tense is the gate to the rest of the semester.

2
I will be able to recognize the regular preterite endings — -é/-aste/-ó/-amos/-aron for -ar, and -í/-iste/-ió/-imos/-ieron for -er/-ir.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You already know these verbs — in the present

In Semester 1 you learned present-tense -ar verbs: yo hablo, tú hablas… The good news is that the past tense uses the same stems — you just swap the endings. So the work today is mostly about new endings on familiar verbs.

Listen, then repeat

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go through the list once more and, for each verb, name one real time you did it: “nadar — last summer at the pool.” Attaching a verb to a real memory is exactly how you'll use it in the past tense this week.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PRETÉRITO REGULAR · LAS FORMAS 10 min

The endings, by themselves

The regular preterite has just two ending sets. Take off the -ar / -er / -ir and add these. Today just meet them — you don't have to produce them yet.

Set 1 — -ar verbs (example: hablar)

SubjectEndingForm
yo-éhablé (I spoke)
tú-astehablaste (you spoke)
usted / él / ella-óhabló (he/she spoke)
nosotros/as-amoshablamos (we spoke)
ustedes / ellos / ellas-aronhablaron (they spoke)

Set 2 — -er / -ir verbs (examples: comer, escribir)

SubjectEndingForm
yo-ícomí / escribí
tú-istecomiste / escribiste
usted / él / ella-iócomió / escribió
nosotros/as-imoscomimos / escribimos
ustedes / ellos / ellas-ieroncomieron / escribieron

Notice the accent marks on yo and él/ella (hablé, habló, comí, comió) — they carry the stress and matter a lot. (Latin-American register: ustedes covers “you all” everywhere; Spain's vosotros is labeled European.)

Now just listen — real examples

Click 🔊 and hear each ending inside a real sentence. Don't translate every word — just listen for the ending doing its job.

  • Ayer hablé…«Ayer hablé con mi familia.» — yo, -ar
  • ¿Hablaste…?«¿Hablaste con el profesor?» — tú, -ar
  • Ella comió…«Ella comió en un restaurante.» — ella, -er
  • Nosotros visitamos…«Nosotros visitamos un museo ayer.» — nosotros, -ar
  • Ellos comieron…«Ellos comieron pescado anoche.» — ellos, -er
✅ PART 4 · A PRACTICAR 11 min

Spot the ending

You're just matching the ending to the subject — the stems are given.

✅ TRY IT · which ending fits yo?
“Yo (viajar) ___ a España.” — it's an -ar verb, subject yo.
✅ TRY IT · which ending fits ella?
“Ella (comer) ___ pescado.” — an -er verb, subject ella.
✅ TRY IT · which ending fits ellos?
“Ellos (bailar) ___ toda la noche.” — an -ar verb, subject ellos.
