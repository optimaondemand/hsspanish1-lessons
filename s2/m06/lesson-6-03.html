★ El pretérito regular — The Full Picture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you met the endings. Today they click into place — both sets, side by side, with the one tricky spot named out loud.
This is the heart of the module. The regular preterite is built from stem + ending: take viajar → viaj-, add -é → viajé (I traveled). You'll see the full -ar set and the full -er/-ir set together, hear each form, and meet the one form that looks identical to the present tense — the nosotros form. Take your time; this is the tense Módulos 7–9 will build on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate regular -ar and -er/-ir verbs in the preterite for all subjects.
2
I will be able to tell the preterite nosotros form apart from the present using time clues.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Stem plus ending

Every regular preterite verb is just two pieces: the stem (the verb minus -ar/-er/-ir) and the ending (which tells you who and when). Say this aloud: visitar → visit- + -é → visité (I visited). That's the whole machine.

🧠 PART 2 · LAS DOS CONJUGACIONES 10 min

Both sets, side by side

-AR → viajar (to travel)

yo viajé
tú viajaste
usted/él/ella viajó
nosotros/as viajamos
ustedes/ellos/ellas viajaron

-ER/-IR → comer (to eat)

yo comí
tú comiste
usted/él/ella comió
nosotros/as comimos
ustedes/ellos/ellas comieron

The shortcut: the two sets share the tú-style shapes and only really differ in the vowel — -ar leans on a (viajaste, viajamos, viajaron), while -er/-ir leans on i (comiste, comimos, comieron). Learn one set well and the other is a small step.

Hear all three types in one line: «Yo caminé, comí y escribí.» (-ar, -er, -ir)

🔊 PART 3 · ESCUCHA Y LA TRAMPA DEL «NOSOTROS» 8 min

Hear the forms in real sentences

  • hablé«Ayer hablé con mi familia.» (yo)
  • hablaste«¿Hablaste con el profesor?» (tú)
  • comió«Ella comió en un restaurante.» (ella)
  • comieron«Ellos comieron pescado anoche.» (ellos)

The one that fools everyone

For -ar verbs, the nosotros preterite looks exactly like the present: viajamos can mean “we travel” or “we traveled.” How do you tell? The time words from Day 1. Listen:

  • visitamos«Nosotros visitamos un museo ayer.» — ayer makes it past

Visitamos un museo ayer = we visited (past, because of ayer). Visitamos museos cada verano = we visit (present, because of cada verano). The verb is the same; the time word decides.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
¿De dónde viene el pretérito?

These very endings arrived from Latin, the language of the Romans who ruled the Iberian Peninsula for centuries — which is why Spanish, Italian, French, and Portuguese all share a family of past-tense endings. When you say viajé, you're using a machine that has been carrying stories about the past for two thousand years, from Roman Hispania to Madrid today.

🌎 CULTURA A FONDO · ESPAÑA
Un país de regiones

España is not one flat culture but many regions, each with its own character — Andalucía in the south with flamenco, the Basque Country in the north with its own ancient language, Cataluña on the Mediterranean coast. Spanish (castellano) is spoken everywhere, but you'll also hear catalán, gallego, and euskera. Remember: in this course our everyday register stays Latin-American (we use ustedes for “you all”); Spain's informal vosotros we simply recognize as European.
