No new grammar — today you pull it back out of your head, and put present against past.

🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · PRESENTE vs. PRETÉRITO 10 min

Same verb, two times

You've used the present all year. Now line it up against the preterite. The stem stays; the ending (and the stress) move. Read across each row and listen to the past form:

VerbPresent (habitual / now)Preterite (finished, past) · listen
hablar yo hablo — I speak / I'm speaking yo hablé — I spoke
comer ella come — she eats ella comió — she ate
visitar nosotros visitamos — we visit nosotros visitamos — we visited

The ear trick: for yo and él/ella, the past pushes the stress to the end — HA-blo (present) becomes ha-BLÉ (past); CO-me becomes co-MIÓ. And remember the nosotros trap: visitamos looks the same in both — only the time word tells you which.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · present or preterite?
«Ayer yo ___ con mi abuela.» (Yesterday I ___ with my grandma.) — the time word is ayer.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · present or preterite?
«Todos los días ella ___ frutas.» (Every day she ___ fruit.) — a habit.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · present or preterite?
«Anoche ellos ___ en la fiesta.» (Last night they ___ at the party.) — bailar, past.
✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check your verb form and spelling.

1. Say: “Yesterday I traveled to Madrid and visited a museum.”

Ayer viajé a Madrid y visité un museo. Both -ar, yo → -é. Ayer sets the past.

2. Say: “Last weekend we danced and sang.”

El fin de semana pasado bailamos y cantamos. Both -ar, nosotros → -amos (the time word makes it past).

3. Say: “Last night she ate fish and I ate a Spanish omelette.”

Anoche ella comió pescado y yo comí una tortilla española. -er: ella → -ió, yo → -í.

You met this on Day 5. Press play, follow along, and this time count how many preterite verbs you can spot.

Play the reading

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Contar el pasado

Every culture loves a good travel story, and Spanish speakers are no exception — from a family recounting «el verano pasado» to friends comparing weekends over tapas. The preterite is the tense of exactly that: finished events, told after the fact. The more of these verbs you own, the more of other people's stories you'll understand.

🌎 ESPAÑA A FONDO
El horario español

One reason Spain's stories often run late into the night: the Spanish clock. Lunch (la comida) is the big meal, often around 2–3 p.m., and dinner (la cena) can start at 9 or 10 p.m. When our narrator says «por la noche cené en un restaurante,» “night” really means night. Daily rhythms like this are part of what makes travel — and telling about it afterward — memorable.
