Lesson 7.03
★ El pretérito irregular — One Pattern, Three Verbs
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Three more irregular verbs — and the good news is they all march to the same drummer.
Yesterday: ir/ser (fui, fue). Today the three workhorses of any travel story: hacer (to do/make → hice), tener (to have → tuve), and estar (to be → estuve). They're “irregular,” but here's the secret that makes them easy: each one has a special stem you memorize once, and then they all take the exact same endings — endings with no accent marks on the yo and él/ella forms. Learn the pattern, and three verbs fall at once.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate hacer, tener, and estar in the preterite using the shared irregular pattern.
2
I will be able to tell what I did, had, and where I was on a trip.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Hear the whole story first

Before the tables, listen to today's three verbs living together in one sentence about a trip to Perú:

  • Fui a Perú…«Fui a Perú, hice una excursión y estuve muy contento.»

That one sentence has fui (I went), hice (I did/made), and estuve (I was) — the whole module in a breath. By the end of today you'll build sentences just like it.

🧠 PART 2 · UN PATRÓN, TRES VERBOS 14 min

Step 1: the shared endings

Every one of today's verbs (and fui from yesterday) uses this same set of endings. Notice: no accent marks on yo or él/ella. That's the giveaway that a verb is a “strong” irregular preterite.

SubjectShared ending
yo-e  (no accent!)
tú-iste
usted / él / ella-o  (no accent!)
nosotros/as-imos
ustedes / ellos / ellas-ieron

Step 2: attach each verb's special stem

Learn one stem per verb, then bolt on the endings above:

VerbStemyoél/ellaellos
hacer (to do/make)hic-hicehizohicieron
tener (to have)tuv-tuvetuvotuvieron
estar (to be)estuv-estuveestuvoestuvieron

One tiny spelling twist: hacer's él/ella form is hizo with a z (not hico) — Spanish swaps c→z before o to keep the soft sound. Everything else is perfectly regular to the pattern.

📚 PART 3 · ESCUCHA LAS FORMAS 8 min

Say each one back

Click 🔊 and repeat. These are the exact model sentences you'll build from all week.

  • hice«Hice muchas fotos en las ruinas.» (I took/made many photos in the ruins.)
  • tuve«Tuve un día maravilloso.» (I had a wonderful day.)
  • estuve«Estuve en las montañas una semana.» (I was in the mountains for a week.)

Note: in Spanish, you hacer a photo (hice una foto) — “made” a photo — where English “takes” one. A small, useful difference.

✅ PART 4 · A PRACTICAR 9 min

Pick the right form

✅ TRY IT · hacer, tener, or estar?
"Yo ___ una excursión a Machu Picchu." (I did/went on an excursion to Machu Picchu.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the form
"Nosotros ___ en el hotel tres noches." (We were in the hotel three nights.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the form
"Mi hermana ___ un problema con su pasaporte." (My sister had a problem with her passport.)

Your turn: how would you say "They took a lot of photos" (they made…)? Say it out loud, then reveal.

Hicieron muchas fotos. "They" → hicieron (stem hic- + -ieron). Remember: you make photos in Spanish.
🌎 CULTURA · LOS ANDES
El techo de Sudamérica

La cordillera de los Andes es la cadena de montañas más larga del mundo — recorre siete países, desde Venezuela hasta Chile. In Perú, the Andes hold Cusco, once the capital of the Inca empire, and the famous ruins of Machu Picchu. Muchos turistas hacen una excursión a las ruinas y estuvieron a más de 2.400 metros de altura — the thin mountain air is part of the aventura.

🌎 CULTURA · A FONDO · MACHU PICCHU Y LOS INCAS
Una ciudad en las nubes

The Inca built Machu Picchu around 1450 without iron tools, the wheel, or mortar — stones cut so precisely a knife blade won't fit between them. Hidden on a ridge, it was never found by Spanish conquistadors and stayed largely unknown to the outside world until 1911. Many Peruvians in the Andes still speak quechua, the language of the Inca; words like cóndor, puma, and even llama traveled from quechua into Spanish and then into English. When you visit Perú this module, you're stepping into a living history, not just old ruins.
