TODAY'S FOCUS
Welcome back. This module's big question: «¿Qué tiempo hace, y qué voy a hacer»? — What's the weather like, and what am I going to do?
Semester 2 opens by bringing everything back. You already own ser, estar, tener, gustar and a room full of present-tense verbs — this module reactivates all of it and then adds one new tool. Today we start with the easy, useful half: the weather and the seasons. There's no new grammar today on purpose. We're stocking up on words — and reawakening the Semester-1 Spanish you'll build on all week. Then you'll open the interactive map and explore the Cono Sur — Argentina and its neighbors, where this module's reading takes you.

IIEL CLIMA Y LAS ESTACIONES14 min

Listen, then repeat

Click the 🔊 and say each expression back out loud. Notice the pattern: most weather is hace + a noun (literally "it makes…"): hace (it makes) is a form of the verb hacer (to do / to make). Two are single verbs: llueve (it rains) and nieva (it snows). Learn them as whole set expressions — don't translate word by word.

🌎 CULTURA · EL CONO SUR
Cuatro países, un extremo del mundo (Four countries, one end of the world)

El Cono Sur — Argentina, Chile, Uruguay y Paraguay — is the southern tip of South America, so far south that Chile's Patagonia nearly touches Antarctica. Because it's below the equator, the whole region runs on flipped seasons: enero is summer, julio is winter. The Andes run down Argentina's western edge, and just across them lies Chile — a long, thin country pressed between mountains and the Pacific.
