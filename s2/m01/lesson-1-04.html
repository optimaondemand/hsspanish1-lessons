IPARA EMPEZAR5 min

The formula, in one breath

Before you write, say the formula out loud: a form of ir (voy/vas/va/vamos/van) + a + an unchanged verb. The form of ir matches the subject; the second verb stays in its "to…" shape. Hold that in mind — you're about to build it yourself.

IIESCRITURA GUIADA14 min

You build the future

Type into the box after each sentence to fill the blank with the correct form of ir a (voy a, vas a, va a, vamos a, van a). The verb after it is already in place — don't change it. Say why to yourself as you write. Commit to all six before you reveal the key.

voy a · vas a · va a · vamos a · van a  +  infinitive
  1. Yo ______ estudiar esta noche.
  2. Nosotros ______ viajar en verano.
  3. Mi hermana ______ ir a la playa mañana.
  4. ¿Tú ______ practicar español hoy?
  5. Ellos ______ comer en casa luego.
  6. Cuando llueve, yo ______ leer en casa.
Accents are part of the word. Click in a box, then click a letter to add it.
1. voy a — "yo" → voy. (Yo voy a estudiar esta noche.)
2. vamos a — "nosotros" → vamos. (Nosotros vamos a viajar en verano.)
3. va a — "mi hermana" (ella) → va. (Mi hermana va a ir a la playa.)
4. vas a — "tú" → vas. (¿Tú vas a practicar español hoy?)
5. van a — "ellos" → van. (Ellos van a comer en casa luego.)
6. voy a — "yo" → voy. (Cuando llueve, yo voy a leer en casa.)

VA PRODUCIR8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check your verb choice and spelling.

1. Say: “Today it's sunny, so I'm going to go to the park.”

Hoy hace sol, entonces voy a ir al parque. Weather (hace sol) then a plan (voy a ir). Note al = a + el.

2. Say: “It's cold and my family is going to eat at home.”

Hace frío y mi familia va a comer en casa. "Mi familia" (ella) → va a + comer.

3. Say: “I'm from the U.S., I like summer, and this weekend I'm going to travel.”

Soy de los Estados Unidos, me gusta el verano, y este fin de semana voy a viajar. Three tools in one breath: ser (origin), gustar (what I like), ir a (plan).
🇦🇷 CULTURA A FONDO · ARGENTINA

A festival in Argentina might be Carnaval in the northern provinces, the grape-harvest Fiesta de la Vendimia in Mendoza, or a neighborhood peña with live folk music and dancing. Festivals across the Spanish-speaking world are where a city shows its heart — and arriving with a few planning phrases («Voy a ver el desfile» (I'm going to see the parade), «Vamos a bailar») turns you from a spectator into a participant.
