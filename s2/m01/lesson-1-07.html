IPARA EMPEZAR5 min

A surprise about the letter V

Here's something that trips up English speakers: in Spanish, the letter v is not the buzzy English "v." It sounds almost exactly like a soft b. So voy sounds like "boy," and verano starts like "beh-." Once you know that, half of this module's key words sound right instantly.

IILA LETRA V (= B)10 min

V sounds like a soft B

Don't press your top teeth to your lip the way you do in English "victory." Just barely touch your lips together, like a gentle b. Listen, then echo — every one of these is a word you know:

  • voy"boy" — soft b, not buzzy v
  • vas"bahs" — same soft b
  • el veranobeh-RA-no
  • hace vientoviento starts like a soft b
  • el inviernoin-BYER-no
IIIEL SONIDO «LL»9 min

Double L makes a "y" sound

In most of Latin America, ll sounds like the English y in "yes." (In much of Argentina you'll actually hear it closer to "sh" — a regional signature — but "y" is understood everywhere.) Listen and echo:

  • llueve"YWEH-veh" — ll = y
  • la playaPLA-ya — ll = y

The word luego starts with a single l — a plain "l," not a "y." Say llueve and luego back to back and hear the difference, then reveal a tip.

One l = an English "l" (luego = "LWEH-go"). Double ll = a "y" (llueve). The doubled letter is its own sound — not just a louder "l."
IVLOS DIPTONGOS6 min

Two vowels, one smooth glide

When two vowels sit together — like ie or ue — glide smoothly from one to the other in a single syllable. Don't chop them apart. Listen:

  • viento → "BYEN-to" (ie glides together)
  • invierno → "in-BYER-no"
  • llueve → "YWEH-veh" (ue glides together)
  • nieva → "NYEH-va"

Tip: say them a little fast at first — the speed forces the glide, then slow down while keeping it smooth.

🇦🇷 CULTURA · EL ACENTO ARGENTINO
El «sheismo» porteño

Listen to someone from Buenos Aires say playa or llueve and you may hear "SHA-sha" and "SHU-eh-veh" — the ll and y pronounced like the English "sh." This «sheísmo» is one of the most recognizable features of Argentine Spanish. You don't need to copy it — the standard "y" is understood everywhere — but now you'll recognize it as the sound of the Río de la Plata.

🇦🇷 CULTURA A FONDO · ARGENTINA

Recording yourself in a new language can feel awkward — and that feeling is universal. Argentine actors, singers, and football commentators all started with a first shaky take. Everyone's early recordings feel strange; that's not a sign you're doing it wrong, it's a sign you're doing it at all. Rehearse, breathe, and press record. ¡Vos podés! (You can do it!)
