Lesson 1.03
★ Ir a + Verb — Talking About the Future
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Three little pieces — a form of ir, the word a, and a verb — and suddenly you can talk about the future.
This is the one new structure of the module: a form of ir + a + an infinitive (a verb in its "to…" form). Voy a estudiar = I'm going to study. English does exactly the same thing ("going to…"), so the idea is already familiar — you just plug in yesterday's five forms of ir. This is how Spanish speakers talk about the near future every day.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to build the near future with ir a + an infinitive (Voy a nadar).
2
I will be able to say what I'm going to do depending on the weather (Si llueve, voy a leer).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

You already say this in English

Think about how you talk about tomorrow: "I'm going to sleep in," "we're going to watch a movie." That "going to + verb" is exactly the structure Spanish uses — with ir. Once you see it, you'll spot it everywhere.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
What's one thing you're going to do later today?
🧠 PART 2 · LA FÓRMULA 12 min

The formula: ir + a + infinitive

voy / vas / va / vamos / van  +  a  +  infinitive
= "am / are / is going to… [do something]"

An infinitive is a verb in its dictionary form — the "-ar / -er / -ir" form you learned last semester: estudiar (to study), comer (to eat), viajar (to travel). You don't change the infinitive; the form of ir does all the work. Listen:

SpanishEnglishListen
Yo voy a estudiar mañana.I'm going to study tomorrow.
¿Vas a ir a la playa?Are you going to go to the beach?
Ella va a comer con su familia.She's going to eat with her family.
Nosotros vamos a practicar español.We're going to practice Spanish.
Ellos van a viajar en verano.They're going to travel in summer.

Notice the a never disappears — it's the glue between ir and the action. Voy a estudiar, not "Voy estudiar."

🌬️ PART 3 · EL CLIMA DECIDE EL PLAN 10 min

Weather sets the plan

Now combine both halves of the module. The weather (Day 1) tells you the plan (ir a + verb). Listen to how one sentence can hold both today and tomorrow:

  • Hoy hace sol…«Hoy hace sol, pero mañana va a llover.» (Today it's sunny, but tomorrow it's going to rain.)
  • Yo voy al parque…«Yo voy al parque cuando hace buen tiempo.» (I go to the park when the weather is nice.)

Two useful phrases you just heard: hace buen tiempo (the weather is nice) and hace mal tiempo (the weather is bad). Try: Cuando hace mal tiempo, voy a leer en casa.

Vamos a viajar en verano. "We" → vamos; then a; then the unchanged infinitive viajar. En verano = in summer.
🇦🇷 CULTURA · ARGENTINA
Las estaciones al revés

Here's something that surprises new learners: in Argentina the seasons are flipped. Because it's south of the equator, July is winter and January is summer. So while students in the U.S. are at the beach in July, a family in Buenos Aires might say «Hoy hace frío; vamos a tomar mate en casa.» When you plan with ir a, the season word matters — and it depends on which hemisphere you're in.

🇦🇷 CULTURA A FONDO · ARGENTINA

In Argentina, the voseo is everywhere: instead of tú, Argentines usually say vos — ¿Vos vas a venir? You'll still be understood perfectly with tú, and this course teaches the pan-Latin-American tú, but if you watch an Argentine film or listen to a porteño (someone from Buenos Aires), that vos is a friendly signature of the region — the same way y'all marks the American South.
