🔈 PART 2 · REPASO DE FONÉTICA 12 min

Three habits + one accent that changes meaning

Your accent rests on three habits: pure, short vowels (no gliding); predictable stress (a written accent overrides the default); and the ñ (ny, as in «mañana»). For the capstone, one more matters:

Without accentWith accentWhy it matters
hablo (I speak — now)habló (he/she spoke — past)the accent moves the meaning to the past
viajo (I travel)viajó / viajé (traveled)same verb, different clock

Listen for the punched final syllable in these preterites:

  • habléstress on the final -É
  • comióstress on the final -Ó
  • …viajé a Méxicohear present, then the punched preterite

Where does the stress fall in caminé? Say it, then reveal.

ca-mi-NÉ — the written accent pulls the stress to the last syllable. That final punch is how a listener hears «I walked» instead of «I walk».
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Record yourself on your phone saying five preterite verbs (hablé, comí, fui, visité, caminé) and play it back. Can you hear the final-syllable punch each time? That playback habit is the fastest way to sharpen your own accent.
🎤 PART 3 · TAREA ORAL 18 min

Your capstone recording (15 points)

Record a self-narrative of about 40–60 seconds with all three moves:

  1. A past experience — at least two preterite verbs (something you did / a trip or day).
  2. A description — use the present with ser/estar and adjectives (a place, or how you felt).
  3. A future plan — one ir a + infinitive (what you're going to do next).
📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. Normalmente vivo en Florida y estudio mucho. El verano pasado viajé a Puerto Rico con mi familia. Caminé por el Viejo San Juan y comí comida deliciosa. La ciudad es bonita y yo estaba muy contento. El próximo año voy a viajar otra vez.»
(Two preterites — viajé, caminé, comí; a description — es bonita, contento; a plan — voy a viajar.)

Hear the model verbs one more time before you record:

  • present → preterite (a trip)
  • voy a… (a future plan)

🎤 CULTURA A FONDO · LA VOZ
Accents you'll hear out in the world

There is no single «correct» Spanish accent — a Mexican, an Argentine, and a Colombian all sound different, and all are perfect Spanish. In much of the Caribbean, final -s softens (¿cómo está(s)?); in Argentina, ll and y sound like «sh». Your own accent will always carry a trace of where you learned — and that's welcome. What matters is being clear and being understood. Speak like yourself, in Spanish.
