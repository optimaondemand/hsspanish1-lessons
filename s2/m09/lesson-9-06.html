📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Week 2 opens with the most useful kind of studying: pulling it all back out of your head.
The graded work in this capstone is cumulative — it can draw on anything from the whole year. So today is a broad spiral: an ear-quiz on travel and everyday vocabulary, a production round, and a table that lines up your major tools side by side — ser/estar, the present, reflexives, and the preterite. Retrieval feels harder than re-reading, and that difficulty is exactly what makes it work.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize spoken vocabulary from across the year and match it to meaning.
2
I will be able to choose the right tool — present, preterite, ser/estar, reflexive — for a given sentence.

Tool (from)Its jobExample · listen

✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 6 min

Build it yourself, then check

Say each full Spanish sentence out loud before you reveal. Being honest with yourself is where the value is.

1. Say: “I am from the United States and my city is big.”

Soy de los Estados Unidos y mi ciudad es grande. Origin → soy; description → es.

2. Say: “Last year I traveled and I visited a museum.”

El año pasado viajé y visité un museo. Two regular preterites — accents on the é.

3. Say: “Tomorrow I'm going to rest.”

Mañana voy a descansar. Near future → ir a + infinitive.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
String all three together into one little paragraph and say it aloud twice. That's a preview of both your capstone recording (Day 8) and the Grammar Quiz (Day 10).
🎉 CULTURA A FONDO · CONTAR HISTORIAS
La sobremesa

Across the Spanish-speaking world, the meal doesn't end when the plates are cleared. The sobremesa — the long conversation that lingers at the table — is where families and friends narrate their day, retell trips, and share news. It's a whole culture built around exactly the skill you're practicing: describing, narrating, and looking ahead, all in one relaxed conversation. When you tell your capstone story, you're joining a very old tradition.
