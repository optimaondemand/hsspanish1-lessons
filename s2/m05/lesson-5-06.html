No new grammar today — just pulling it back out of your head, which is where real learning happens.
First day of Week 2. Instead of new material, today you retrieve: an ear-quiz on the food words, a short production task, and a spiral back to Semester 1 — the verb gustar (to like) and the food you already knew (el arroz, el pollo, la fruta, las verduras). 🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · GUSTAR + LA COMIDA 10 min

Bring back gustar from Semester 1

Remember gustar? It works “backwards”: use gusta for one thing and gustan for more than one. Pair it with the food you know — old and new:

To say…Use…Example · listen
I like one food Me gusta + singular Me gusta el pescado.
I like plural foods Me gustan + plural Me gustan las verduras.
the verb itself gustar (to be pleasing) gustar · comer

Now combine both worlds: someone asks about a food, and you answer with a pronoun instead of repeating it.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · gusta or gustan?
"Me ___ el arroz y el pollo." (I like rice and chicken.)
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · which pronoun?
"¿Comes fruta?" → "Sí, ___ como todos los días." (the fruit → a pronoun)
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · which pronoun?
"¿Preparas el pollo?" → "Sí, ___ preparo con arroz." (the chicken → a pronoun)
✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. This is a self-test, so being honest with yourself is where the value is.

1. Say: “I like fish, and I know how to cook it.” (use gustar, saber, and a pronoun)

Me gusta el pescado, y sé cocinarlo. (Or …y lo sé cocinar.) Gusta for one thing; sé + infinitive for a skill; lo replaces el pescado.

2. Say: “I know a good supermarket; I buy vegetables there.” (use conocer + a pronoun)

Conozco un buen supermercado; allí las compro. A place → conocer; las replaces las verduras.

3. Say: “I like desserts, but they are expensive.”

Me gustan los postres, pero son caros. Plural → gustan; the adjective caros agrees with los postres.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
¿Qué te gusta comer?

Ask a Spaniard what they like and you'll hear the same foods on your list: Me gusta el jamón, me gustan las aceitunas (olives), me gusta el queso manchego. Spain's cooking is regional — seafood on the coasts, hearty stews inland — but everywhere it's built from simple, fresh ingredients bought that morning.

👍 CULTURA A FONDO · ESPAÑA
El horario de las comidas

Spain eats on a famously late clock. El desayuno (breakfast) is light — coffee and toast. La comida, the main meal, comes around 2–3 p.m. And la cena (dinner) is often as late as 9 or 10 p.m. Between them, many people stop for tapas or a merienda (afternoon snack).

This late rhythm surprises many visitors, but it fits the Spanish day: shops may close midday, and evenings run long. When you order in the VR mercado later this semester, that easy, unhurried pace is part of what you'll step into.
