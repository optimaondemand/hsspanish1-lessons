★ En el restaurante — New Words & New Verb Forms
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
From the market to the restaurant — plus the two verbs that both mean "to know."
Today has two jobs. First, restaurant words: the menu, the bill, the waiter, and the verbs you'll use — comprar, pagar, probar. Then we meet the forms of two important new verbs, saber and conocer. Both translate as "to know" in English, but Spanish keeps them separate. Today we just learn the forms and hear them; tomorrow (Lesson 5.03) we dig into when to use each — and meet a second grammar idea, the direct-object pronouns.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two kinds of "knowing"

In English, "know" does two very different jobs. You know a fact ("I know the answer") and you know a person ("I know Ana"). Spanish uses two separate verbs for these — and once you see the split, it feels natural. Today just meet them; tomorrow you'll master the choice.

📚 PART 2 · EN EL RESTAURANTE 13 min

Listen, then repeat

Click the 🔊 and say each word back. The first three are things in a restaurant; the next three are verbs you'll use to shop and order. The last two — saber and conocer — are the "to know" verbs whose forms you'll study in Part 3.

  • el menúthe menu
  • la cuentathe bill / check
  • el camarerothe waiter
  • comprarto buy
  • pagarto pay
  • probarto try / to taste
  • saberto know (facts / how to)
  • conocerto know (people / places)

Note: in Spain a waiter is el camarero; across much of Latin America you'll also hear el mesero. Both are correct — we use camarero since our featured country is España.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Imagine you're at a café in Sevilla. Say the four steps of a meal out loud in order: Miro el menú. El camarero llega. Quiero probar la tortilla. Pido la cuenta y pago. Acting out the sequence makes the words stick.
🧠 PART 3 · LAS FORMAS: SABER & CONOCER 14 min

The forms — just the shapes for now

Both are regular -er verbs in most forms — except the yo form, which is irregular in each. Those two yo forms are the ones to memorize: sé and conozco.

Subjectsaberconocer
yoséconozco
túsabesconoces
usted / él / ellasabeconoce
nosotros/assabemosconocemos
ustedes / ellos / ellassabenconocen

Listen to the three yo/tú forms in real sentences — say each one back:

  • Yo sé cocinar.saber + infinitive = to know how to
  • Yo conozco un buen restaurante.conocer = to be familiar with a place
  • ¿Sabes dónde está el mercado?saber = to know a fact / information

Sabemos cocinar. Saber + infinitive = to know how to do something; nosotros → sabemos. Tomorrow you'll learn exactly when to reach for saber vs. conocer.
🌎 CULTURA · SEVILLA
El Mercado de Triana

In Sevilla, one of the best places to buy food is the Mercado de Triana, a covered market across the river where vendors sell fresh pescado, queso, tomate, and fruta. Shoppers there don't just comprar — they prueban (taste) samples, chat with the camareros at the little tapas bars inside, and pay la cuenta with a friendly word. A market is as much about conversation as about food.
