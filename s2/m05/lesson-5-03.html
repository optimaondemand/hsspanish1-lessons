★ Two Ways to "Know" & a Shortcut for "It"
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two grammar tools today — and both make you sound much more natural.
First, the big choice: saber vs. conocer. Both mean "to know," but saber is for facts and skills and conocer is for being familiar with people and places. Second, direct-object pronouns (lo, la, los, las) — little words that stand in for a thing you've already named, so you don't repeat it: instead of «Compro la fruta» you can answer «La compro.» Both ideas come back all module long.

1
I will be able to choose between saber (facts/skills) and conocer (people/places).
2
I will be able to replace a named thing with lo, la, los, or las to avoid repeating it.

🧠 PART 2 · ¿SABER O CONOCER? 13 min

The one rule that matters

SABER — facts & skills

Information, facts, and (with an infinitive) knowing how to do something.

Sé cocinar. (I know how to cook.)
¿Sabes dónde está el mercado? (Do you know where the market is?)
Sabemos la receta. (We know the recipe.)

CONOCER — people & places

Being familiar with a person, place, or thing.

Conozco un buen restaurante. (I know a good restaurant.)
¿Conoces a Ana? (Do you know Ana?)
Conocemos Sevilla. (We know / have been to Sevilla.)

The shortcut: if you can add how to, a fact, or information → saber. If it's a person, a place, or something you're familiar with → conocer. One more rule: with a specific person, conocer takes the personal a — Conozco a Ana.

🧠 PART 3 · LO / LA / LOS / LAS 13 min

A shortcut so you don't repeat yourself

When you've already named a thing, you don't repeat it — you swap in a little pronoun for "it" or "them." Which one you pick depends on the gender and number of the thing it replaces. The pronoun usually goes right before the conjugated verb.

PronounReplaces…Example
loa masculine singular thing (el postre)¿El postre? Lo quiero.
laa feminine singular thing (la fruta)¿La fruta? La compro.
losa masculine / mixed plural (los huevos)¿Los huevos? Los pongo aquí.
lasa feminine plural (las manzanas)¿Las manzanas? Las como.

Listen to three real answers. Notice the pronoun matches the food it replaces — and comes before the verb:

  • ¿La fruta? La compro en el mercado.la = la fruta (fem. sing.)
  • ¿Los huevos? Los pongo en la mesa.los = los huevos (masc. pl.)
  • Quiero probar el postre. Lo quiero ahora.lo = el postre (masc. sing.)

Good to know: with an infinitive you may also attach the pronoun to the end — «Quiero probarlo» means the same as «Lo quiero probar.» Either is correct.

✅ TRY IT · which pronoun?
«¿La carne? ___ compro en el supermercado.» (The meat? I buy ___ at the supermarket.)
✅ TRY IT · which pronoun?
«¿El queso? ___ pruebo primero.» (The cheese? I taste ___ first.)

Your turn: answer «¿Compras las manzanas?» using a pronoun (say “Yes, I buy them”). Say it, then reveal.

Sí, las compro. Las manzanas is feminine plural → las; it sits right before the verb compro.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
La tortilla española

Spain's most famous everyday dish is the tortilla española — a thick omelette of huevos, potatoes, and onion, served in wedges hot or cold. (Careful: in Spain a tortilla is this omelette, not the flat Mexican bread — a great example of how one word can mean different foods in different countries.) Ask a Spaniard «¿Sabes hacer tortilla?» and you're using today's grammar: saber for a skill.

🍽️ CULTURA A FONDO · ESPAÑA
La cultura de las tapas

A tapa is a small plate of food — a few olives, a slice of queso, a bit of pescado — served with a drink. In Sevilla and much of southern Spain, going out for tapas (ir de tapas) means moving from bar to bar with friends, sharing many small dishes instead of one big meal. It's social, unhurried, and built around conversation.

The word itself tells a story: tapa means "lid" or "cover," and one legend says bartenders once laid a slice of bread or ham over a glass to keep flies out — the free snack that "covered" the drink became a whole way of eating. Notice today's grammar hiding in the custom: you conoces a good tapas bar (a place), and you sabes which tapa to order (information).
