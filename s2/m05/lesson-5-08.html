⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

One letter, two sounds

Spanish spelling is regular, but the letter c plays two roles depending on the vowel that follows it. Get that rule down and a whole set of food words falls into place. Let's name the challenge first.

📚 PART 2 · C, Z Y QU 12 min

The rule, then the food words

SpellingSoundExample
c + a, o, uhard “k”carne, cocina, cuenta
c + e, isoft (see note below)cocina, delcioso
zsame soft sound as soft cmanzana
qu + e, ihard “k” (the u is silent)queso, ¿qué quieres?

The Spain vs. Latin America difference: in most of Spain, soft c (before e/i) and z are pronounced like the th in English "think" — so manzana sounds like “man-tha-na.” In Latin America (and in the south of Spain, including Sevilla), the same letters sound like a plain s — “man-sa-na.” Both are correct. Use whichever you like — just be consistent.

Model words — listen, then echo

  • la cocinahard co (k) + soft ci
  • el quesoqu = hard “k,” the u is silent
  • la manzanathe z sound — listen closely
  • la cuentacu = “kw” before a vowel
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say this tongue-twister slowly, then faster: El camarero cocina queso, carne y cinco manzanas caras. Every c, z, and qu in one breath. Record it on your phone and play it back — the fastest way to hear your own sounds.
🧠 PART 3 · EL ACENTO EN LA COMIDA 8 min

Where does the stress land?

Two quick reminders on stress with this module's words:

  • Words ending in a vowel, -n, or -s stress the second-to-last syllable: man-ZA-na, to-MA-te, car-NE… wait — CAR-ne (two syllables).
  • A written accent beats the default: me-NÚ ends in a vowel, so it would normally be stressed ME-nu — the accent pulls it to the end, me-NÚ.
  • el menúaccent → stress the last syllable, -NÚ
  • el postreends in a vowel → POS-tre

The word camarero ends in a vowel. Say it aloud and decide which syllable you punch — then reveal.

Stress the second-to-last syllable: ca-ma-RE-ro. It ends in a vowel, so the default rule applies — no accent mark needed.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El ceceo y el seseo

The “th” sound for c and z is one of the most recognizable features of European Spanish — but it isn't universal even within Spain. In much of Andalucía, including Sevilla, people use seseo (the s sound), just like Latin America. So a sevillano and a Mexican may pronounce manzana the same way, while a madrileño says it with “th.” None is “more correct” — it's simply regional variety, and it's part of what makes Spanish a global language.

💬 CULTURA A FONDO · ESPAÑA
Pedir en un restaurante

In a Spanish restaurant, a few courteous phrases go a long way. To get the waiter's attention: «Perdone / Disculpe» (excuse me). To order: «Para mí, la tortilla, por favor» (for me, the omelette, please) or «Quería probar…» (I'd like to try…). To finish: «La cuenta, por favor» — or, informally, a small hand gesture as if signing the air.

Notice you already own the grammar behind ordering: ¿Qué me recomienda? uses a question word; «La quiero probar» uses a pronoun; «Sé que está rica» uses saber. You'll use these exact moves in today's Tarea oral.

🎤 PART 4 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself speaking Spanish for about 30–45 seconds. Choose one of the two options below. Whichever you pick, include at least one saber or conocer and one direct-object pronoun (lo/la/los/las).

Option A — En el restaurante

Role-play ordering a meal: greet the camarero, ask for the menú, order a food, and ask for la cuenta. Use a pronoun («La quiero probar»).

Option B — Sé cocinar

Talk about a food you know how to make: say what it is, that you sabes cocinarlo, where you buy the ingredients, and answer with a pronoun («Los compro en…»).
📝 A MODEL (Option B — yours must be true for you)

«Hola. Sé preparar una tortilla española. Necesito huevos y queso; los compro en el supermercado. Conozco un buen mercado cerca de mi casa. ¡La tortilla es muy rica!»
(One saber, one conocer, and the pronoun los for the eggs.)

Hear the target structures one more time before you record:

  • Yo sé cocinar.saber + infinitive
  • ¿Los huevos? Los pongo en la mesa.a pronoun in action
  • Sé la receta, pero no conozco al chef.both verbs, side by side
