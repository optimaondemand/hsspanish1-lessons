🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Un país de regiones

España is made of distinct regions, each with its own food and character: Andalucía in the south (Sevilla, flamenco, gazpacho), Cataluña in the northeast (Barcelona), the País Vasco with its famous pintxos, and Galicia in the northwest, known for seafood. Some regions even have their own languages alongside Spanish — catalán, euskera, gallego. One country, many tables.

🥾 PART 3 · VISTA PREVIA DE LA VR · ENGAGE 14 min

Un viaje por el mundo hispano — your semester VR project

Later this semester you'll put on a headset and step into ENGAGE, our VR platform, for the Semester 2 project: Un viaje por el mundo hispano (A journey through the Spanish-speaking world). You'll arrive in a Spanish-speaking city during a festival and move through a whole trip in Spanish. You're not performing today. This is your preview, so that when the session comes you already know the moves.

🎓 WHAT YOU'LL DO IN THE PROJECT
  1. Llegar — arrive in a city during a festival. Take in the plaza, the music, the food stalls.
  2. Pedir y seguir direcciones — ask for and follow directions to get around (skills from earlier this semester).
  3. Buscar alojamiento — arrange lodging. Ask about a room, prices, what's included.
  4. Pedir comida — order food at a stall or restaurant — exactly this module's language (el menú, la cuenta, probar).
  5. Contar lo que hiciste — recount what you did. At the end you'll tell the story of your day — which needs a past tense… and that's precisely what Módulo 6 teaches next.

🎉 CULTURA A FONDO · ESPAÑA
Las fiestas de España

Spain is famous for its festivals, and food is at the center of almost every one. Sevilla holds the spring Feria de Abril, a week of dancing, horses, and casetas (tents) serving tapas and rebujito. Valencia burns giant sculptures at Las Fallas; Buñol throws tomatoes at La Tomatina; and all across the country towns celebrate their patron saint with street food and music.

Your VR project drops you into exactly this kind of scene — a city alive with a festival — so the Spanish you've built (greetings, directions, ordering food) has a real, joyful place to land.
