★ Un día en la CDMX — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 50 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The verbs step out of the drills and into a real story about a real city.
Meet Fernanda, who knows la Ciudad de México by heart. As she describes her morning, you'll see every irregular-yo verb from last week doing an honest day's work — salgo, hago, pongo, doy, veo, conozco. This is your third encounter with these forms, this time as connected language you can actually understand. Then you'll find México on the interactive map, and a culture spotlight takes you deeper into México's enormous, ancient capital.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to understand a short first-person passage that uses irregular-yo verbs and city vocabulary.
2
I will be able to describe a few facts about la Ciudad de México and its daily life.
3
I will be able to locate México on the map and name its capital, la Ciudad de México.

📖 PART 2 · A LEER · FERNANDA EN LA CDMX 14 min

Listen and follow along

Press play, follow the words, then read it again on your own. The bold words are the irregular-yo verbs from this module.

Play the reading

«Me llamo Fernanda y conozco muy bien la Ciudad de México. Cada mañana salgo de mi casa y hago muchas cosas en el centro. Pongo mis libros en la mochila y doy un paseo por la plaza. Veo el mercado, las tiendas y los museos. Es una ciudad enorme y maravillosa.»

Glosario: cada (each / every) · muchas cosas (many things) · enorme (huge / enormous) · maravillosa (wonderful)

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Point to each bold verb and say the infinitive it comes from: conozco ← conocer, salgo ← salir, hago ← hacer, pongo ← poner, doy ← dar, veo ← ver. Then retell the whole thing about Fernanda (change yo → él): «Él conoce la ciudad, sale de casa…»

🌎 CULTURA · EL MERCADO
Comprar en el mercado

When Fernanda says «veo el mercado,» she means something specific: a bustling covered market full of stalls (puestos). México's markets, like the famous Mercado de la Merced, sell everything from chiles and fruit to flowers and hand-made crafts (artesanías). Bargaining is normal, and vendors greet regulars by name. It's a very different rhythm from a supermarket — slower, louder, and full of conversation.

🇲🇽 MÉXICO A FONDO · EL CENTRO HISTÓRICO
El corazón de la CDMX

Fernanda's centro is the Centro Histórico of la Ciudad de México — a UNESCO World Heritage site built directly over the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan. There you'll find the giant main square (el Zócalo), the ruins of the Templo Mayor, and the Catedral Metropolitana, all within a few blocks. Nearby, the enormous Bosque de Chapultepec park holds world-class museums like the Museo Nacional de Antropología. When Fernanda says «Veo… los museos», she has more than 150 to choose from — one of the highest counts of any city in the world.

🌎 CULTURA · UN PAÍS ENORME
México de norte a sur

México stretches from the deserts of the north to tropical beaches and jungle in the south. It's the most populous Spanish-speaking country in the world and home to 32 states. Beyond the capital, cities like Guadalajara (home of mariachi), Oaxaca (famous for food and crafts), and Mérida (in the Maya region) each have their own flavor — but everywhere, you can ask «¿Cómo llego a…?» and follow the answer.
