🇲🇽 MÉXICO A FONDO · EL CENTRO HISTÓRICO
El corazón de la CDMX

Fernanda's centro is the Centro Histórico of la Ciudad de México — a UNESCO World Heritage site built directly over the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan. There you'll find the giant main square (el Zócalo), the ruins of the Templo Mayor, and the Catedral Metropolitana, all within a few blocks. Nearby, the enormous Bosque de Chapultepec park holds world-class museums like the Museo Nacional de Antropología. When Fernanda says «Veo… los museos», she has more than 150 to choose from — one of the highest counts of any city in the world.

💡 Why does Tenochtitlan look so strange? It is not a Spanish word. It comes from Náhuatl, the language of the Aztecs, which more than a million people in México still speak today. That is why its spelling looks unusual: the tl sound in Tenochtitlan is everywhere in Náhuatl but rare in Spanish. The name México itself comes from Náhuatl too.

🌎 CULTURA · EL MERCADO
Comprar en el mercado

When Fernanda says «veo el mercado,» she means something specific: a bustling covered market full of stalls (puestos). México's markets, like the famous Mercado de la Merced, sell everything from chiles and fruit to flowers and hand-made crafts (artesanías). Bargaining is normal, and vendors greet regulars by name. It's a very different rhythm from a supermarket — slower, louder, and full of conversation.

🌎 CULTURA · UN PAÍS ENORME
México de norte a sur

México stretches from the deserts of the north to tropical beaches and jungle in the south. It's the most populous Spanish-speaking country in the world and home to 31 states and its capital, Mexico City. Beyond the capital, cities like Guadalajara (home of mariachi), Oaxaca (famous for food and crafts), and Mérida (in the Maya region) each have their own flavor — but everywhere, you can ask «¿Cómo llego a…?» and follow the answer.

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE
Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz (México, born 1648 or 1651, died 1695)

Sor Juana lived and worked right here, in la Ciudad de México. She learned to read at three, by secretly sitting in on her older sister’s lessons. Later, when she set herself something to learn, she cut off a few inches of her hair and gave herself until it grew back to learn it. If she had not learned it by then, she cut her hair again. She tells this story herself, in a letter she wrote in 1691.

People in her time called her la Décima Musa, the Tenth Muse. That is perseverance: steady effort toward something hard, even when nobody is making you do it.

Sor Juana es de México. Vive en la Ciudad de México, estudia mucho y es muy inteligente.
(Sor Juana is from Mexico. She lives in Mexico City, studies a lot, and is very intelligent.)
