★ Fonética — The Spanish R, and Your Voice on the Record
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written these words all week. Today you say them — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic built around the sounds that fill a set of directions: the Spanish r (a soft tap, and a strong trill), and the c / z / qu family. You'll rehearse each with words you already know — derecha, cerca, esquina, izquierda — then record your first graded Tarea oral: give directions to a place in your town, and say what you do downtown.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why sound matters for directions

Directions only work if the listener understands them. The difference between pero and perro, or a clear derecha, can send someone the right way — or the wrong one. Today we lock in the sounds, then you use them on air.

📚 PART 2 · LA R — TOQUE Y VIBRACIÓN 11 min

One letter, two jobs

The Spanish r has two sounds. A single r between vowels is a quick tap — the tongue flicks the roof of the mouth once, like the tt in American English “butter.” A double rr, or an r at the start of a word, is a trill — several taps in a row.

SoundHowWords from this module
r (tap)one quick flickderecha · cerca · mercado · cuadra
rr / r- (trill)several tapsrestaurante · derecho (strong first r) · iz-quier-da

Listen, then echo

Click 🔊 and say each word back, feeling the tongue tap. These are all direction words you'll use in the Tarea oral.

  • a la derechatap the r in de-re-cha
  • derechosame tap — but a different meaning (straight)
  • cercaquick tap: cer-ca
  • el semáforosoft tap: se-má-fo-ro
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say the tongue-twister slowly, then faster: “El perro corre por la carretera.” Feel the trill in perro and carretera versus the single tap in pero. Recording yourself and playing it back is the fastest way to hear your own r.
🧠 PART 3 · LA C, LA Z Y LA QU 9 min

The /k/ and /s/ sounds

In Latin American Spanish:

  • c before a, o, u → a hard /k/: casa, cuadra, conozco.
  • c before e, i → an /s/: centro, cerca.
  • z → also /s/ in Latin America: plaza, izquierda.
  • qu → a /k/ with a silent u: esquina, izquierda.
  • la esquinaes-ki-na (silent u)
  • a la izquierdaiz-kier-da; the z is /s/
  • la plazapla-sa in Latin America

The word conozco has both a /s/ and a /k/ in it. Say it slowly, then reveal.

co-nos-co — the z is /s/ and the final c is /k/: co-nos-ko. That's the special yo form of conocer.
🌎 CULTURA · EL ACENTO MEXICANO
Cómo suena la CDMX

Mexican Spanish is famous for being clear and even-paced — one reason so many films and TV shows are dubbed in México for all of Latin America. Vowels stay crisp, the r is tapped cleanly, and the z and soft c are pronounced as /s/ (never the “th” of central Spain). When you keep your vowels short and tap your r, you're already sounding closer to a chilango.

🇲🇽 MÉXICO A FONDO · PALABRAS NÁHUATL
El español mexicano y el náhuatl

México's Spanish carries thousands of words from Náhuatl, the language of the Aztecs — and many have traveled into English too: chocolate, tomate (tomato), aguacate (avocado), chile, coyote. In the CDMX you'll walk through neighborhoods with Náhuatl names like Chapultepec (“hill of the grasshopper”). Speaking Mexican Spanish means speaking a living blend of two great traditions — something to carry with pride into your recording.

🎤 PART 4 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your first graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself doing two things: (1) give directions to a real place in your town — from your home or school, using derecho, a la derecha / izquierda, la esquina, el semáforo, and cuadras; and (2) say what you do downtown in at least two sentences using irregular-yo verbs (salgo, hago, veo, doy, pongo…). Aim for 30–45 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be about your own town)

«Para llegar al mercado, sigues derecho dos cuadras y doblas a la derecha en el semáforo. En el centro, yo salgo temprano, veo los museos y doy un paseo por la plaza.»
(That's directions to a place, plus three irregular-yo verbs about what I do downtown.)

Need to hear the target verbs one more time before you record? Replay these:

  • Yo salgo…salir — I leave / go out
  • Yo veo…ver — I see
  • Yo doy…dar — I give / take (a stroll)

💡 CONSEJO · plan before you record

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.
