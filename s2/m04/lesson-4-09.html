🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Las Islas Galápagos

About 1,000 km off Ecuador's coast lie the Islas Galápagos, home to giant tortoises, marine iguanas, and blue-footed boobies found nowhere else on Earth. It was here that Charles Darwin gathered the observations behind his theory of evolution. Today the islands are a protected national park — a reminder that Ecuador packs an astonishing range of life into one small country.

🥾 PART 3 · VISTA PREVIA DE LA VR · ENGAGE 14 min

Un viaje por el mundo hispano — your semester VR project

Later this semester you'll put on a headset and step into ENGAGE, our VR platform, for the Quarter 3 project: a journey through a Spanish-speaking city during a festival. You'll arrive somewhere new, find your way around, get settled, eat, and — at the end — tell the story of your day. You're not performing today. This is your preview, so that when the session comes you already know the moves.

🎓 WHAT YOU'LL DO IN THE PROJECT
  1. Llegar a una ciudad y un festival — arrive. Step into a plaza during a local fiesta and greet people.
  2. Pedir direcciones — ask for directions. ¿Dónde está…? ¿Cómo llego a…? Follow the way there.
  3. Buscar alojamiento — find lodging. Check in somewhere to stay for the night.
  4. Pedir comida — order food. Sit down (sentarse) and order something at a café or stall.
  5. Contar lo que hiciste — recount your day. At the end, tell the story of what you did — in order, using sequence words, just like narrating a routine.

Notice that last step: recounting what you did is exactly the narrate-in-order skill you've built all module. The routine you recorded yesterday (primero…, después…) is the same muscle you'll use to tell the story of a day of travel.

Rehearse one line before you ever step in

💭 REHEARSE · no wrong answer — think it through
At the end of the day in VR, you want to say “First I arrived, then I ate.” Which opener sounds most natural?

(You'll learn the full past-tense “I did” forms later this semester — for now, notice how the same sequence words that order a routine also order a story.)

One more rehearsal — ordering food

You sit down at a café stall in the plaza. How would you politely say "I'd like a coffee, please"? Say it aloud, then reveal.

Quiero un café, por favor. (Or, more polite: Me gustaría un café.) And to sit down first: Me siento a la mesa — there's a reflexive you already own.

Sketch a quick “travel-day script” for yourself: write one line for each step of the project — a greeting on arrival, a directions question, a check-in line, a food order, and one sentence recounting the day with primero / después. Keep it; you'll walk into the VR session with your lines ready.
🌎 CULTURA A FONDO · ECUADOR
La Fiesta de la Mama Negra

If your VR journey lands you in Ecuador during a festival, it might be the Mama Negra in the Andean city of Latacunga — a huge, colorful celebration that blends Indigenous, African, and Spanish traditions with costumes, brass bands, and dancing through the streets. Ecuador's festival calendar also includes Inti Raymi, the Andean festival of the sun. Festivals like these are where a city's whole daily routine pauses — and turns into a party.
