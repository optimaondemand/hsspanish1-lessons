IILA LECTURA · Camila21 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud by a native speaker. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know the important ones.

Escuchar la lectura completa — La rutina de Camila

«Hola, me llamo Camila. Soy de Quito, Ecuador, la ciudad en la mitad del mundo. Entre semana me despierto temprano, a las seis, con el despertador. Primero me levanto y me ducho. Después me visto y me cepillo los dientes. Mi hermana se peina y se pone el uniforme. Desayunamos juntas y vamos a la escuela. Por la noche me acuesto tarde porque estudio mucho. Los fines de semana no me levanto temprano; ¡me quedo en la cama!»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

me llamo = my name is · la mitad del mundo = the middle of the world (the equator) · entre semana = on weekdays · temprano = early · el despertador = the alarm clock · los dientes = teeth · el uniforme = the uniform · desayunamos = we eat breakfast · juntas = together · por la noche = at night · tarde = late · los fines de semana = on weekends · me quedo en la cama = I stay in bed

In your own words: why does Camila se acuesta tarde during the week? Write your answer in English, then reveal.

Because she estudia mucho — she studies a lot. So she wakes up early (se despierta temprano) but goes to bed late (se acuesta tarde). Two reflexives, opposite ends of her day.

Tell your day in order, then check

For each prompt, type the full Spanish sentence — use the sequence words to put it in order — then press Check. This is a self-test, so being honest with yourself is where the value is. It's also a dress rehearsal for the Day-7 oral task.

The word key on the right has every word you need: click a word to type it into your box. If something is off, the checker tells you what to fix.

1. Write: “First I wake up, and then I shower.”

Accents are part of the word. Click in a box, then click a letter to add it.
Primero me despierto, y después me ducho. Sequence words primero / después; the e→ie jump in despierto.

2. Write: “Before school, I brush my teeth and get dressed.”

Antes de la escuela, me cepillo los dientes y me visto. Note los dientes (the teeth) — the pronoun me already says they're mine.

3. Write: “My family and I go to bed late on weekends.”

Mi familia y yo nos acostamos tarde los fines de semana. "My family and I" = nosotros → pronoun nos, and NO vowel jump: acostamos.

🔑 WORD KEY

Pronouns
  • me — myself (yo)
  • nos — ourselves (nosotros)
Routine verbs
  • despertarse (e→ie) — to wake up
  • ducharse — to shower
  • cepillarse — to brush
  • vestirse (e→i) — to get dressed
  • acostarse (o→ue) — to go to bed
Pronoun first, then the verb with its normal ending. The vowel jumps in every form except nosotros.
In order
  • primero — first
  • después — then, after
  • antes de — before
  • y — and
Also useful
  • la escuela — school
  • los dientes — teeth
  • tarde — late
  • los fines de semana — on weekends
  • mi familia y yo — my family and I
🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Quito, la mitad del mundo

Camila lives in Quito, the capital of Ecuador and one of the highest capital cities in the world (about 2,850 meters up in the Andes). Its old center is so beautifully preserved that it was one of the very first places named a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Just north of the city, a yellow line marks La Mitad del Mundo — the equator — where visitors take a photo with a foot in each hemisphere.

Because Ecuador sits right on the equator, the sun comes up and goes down at nearly the same time every day of the year. That steady light shapes daily life: routines like Camila's stay remarkably consistent from January to July — there's no long dark winter to sleep through.

🌎 CULTURA A FONDO · ECUADOR
El desayuno ecuatoriano (Ecuadorian)

After Camila se viste, her family sits down to breakfast (el desayuno). In the Ecuadorian highlands that often means humitas (steamed fresh-corn cakes wrapped in husks) or bolones de verde (fried green-plantain balls with cheese), with café or hot colada (a warm, thick drink of grain or fruit). Corn and the plantain have been staples of Andean tables for thousands of years — a daily routine that connects a teenager in Quito to a very long history.

🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
El uniforme escolar (school)

Camila's sister se pone el uniforme — and so do most students across Ecuador and Latin America. School uniforms are the daily norm in both public and private schools, which is why ponerse el uniforme is a real step in a Latin-American teen's morning routine. It's a small cultural difference that changes what "getting dressed for school" actually means.

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT
Santa Mariana de Jesús (Ecuador, 1618–1645)

Mariana de Jesús was born and died in Quito, the city of Camila’s reading. She lost her parents as a small child and grew up in her older sister’s house. She chose to stay there, in one small room, and to live a very simple, strict life that she planned herself: prayer, quiet work and care for others. She read, sewed, wove and embroidered so that no hour was wasted, and she played the guitar and sang. She taught children their prayers and helped people in need.

In 1645 earthquakes shook Quito. Mariana offered her life to God for her city, fell ill and died that same year, at 26. In 1945 Ecuador named her a national heroine, and in 1950 she became Ecuador’s first saint. People call her la Azucena de Quito, the Lily of Quito. That is self-government: being in charge of your own day and your own choices, even when no one is making you.

Mariana de Jesús es de Quito. Ayuda a los niños y a las familias.
(Mariana de Jesús is from Quito. She helps children and families.)
