Lesson 4.03
★ Los verbos reflexivos — The Full Picture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you met the pronoun on a simple verb. Today the whole system snaps together — including the verbs that change in the middle.
You already have the two pieces: the pronoun (me, te, se, nos) and the verb ending. Today you'll see them working across the full set of routine verbs — and meet the twist: a few reflexives also stem-change. Despertarse becomes me despierto and acostarse becomes me acuesto. If that feels familiar, it should — it's the exact same stem-change engine you met with querer and dormir back in Módulo 2.

📚 PART 2 · LA RUTINA EN ACCIÓN 8 min

Listen to whole sentences

These clips put the pronoun and the verb together in real sentences. Click 🔊, say each one back, and listen for where the little pronoun lands — always right in front of the verb.

  • Me lavo…«Me lavo la cara y me cepillo los dientes.» (I wash my face and brush my teeth.)
  • Ella se viste…«Ella se viste rápido.» (She gets dressed quickly.)
  • Nos duchamos…«Nosotros nos duchamos por la mañana.» (We shower in the morning.)

Notice me lavo la cara — in Spanish you say "the face," not "my face." The pronoun me already tells us whose face it is.

🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA COMPLETA 12 min

Two kinds of reflexive verb

Every reflexive uses the pronoun the same way. The only difference is whether the verb also changes a vowel in its stem. Compare a plain one with a stem-changer:

Regular reflexive — levantarse

yo me levanto
tú te levantas
él/ella se levanta
nosotros nos levantamos
ellos se levantan

Stem-changer — despertarse (e→ie)

yo me despierto
tú te despiertas
él/ella se despierta
nosotros nos despertamos
ellos se despiertan

The pattern to notice: the vowel jumps in every form except nosotros — exactly like querer (queremos) and dormir (dormimos) in Módulo 2. The same is true of acostarse (o→ue): me acuesto, but nos acostamos. And vestirse makes an e→i jump: me visto.

🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Quito y la mitad del mundo

Ecuador takes its name from the equator — the line that splits the planet into north and south runs right through it. Just outside the capital, Quito, you can stand at La Mitad del Mundo (“the middle of the world”) with one foot in each hemisphere. Near the equator the sun rises and sets at almost the same time all year, so a Quiteño's daily routine barely shifts between seasons: me despierto and me acuesto at nearly the same hours every month.

🌎 CULTURA A FONDO · ECUADOR
Un país, cuatro mundos

Ecuador is small on the map but astonishingly varied: within a single country you can move from the Costa (Pacific coast) to the Sierra (the high Andes, where Quito sits at almost 2,850 meters), down to the Amazon rainforest, and out to the famous Islas Galápagos, where Charles Darwin studied the wildlife. A morning routine in the cool Andes and one on the hot coast can look very different — and both are Ecuadorian.
