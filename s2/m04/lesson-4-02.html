★ Primero, después… — Sequence Words & the Reflexive Pronoun
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned the actions. Today you learn how to order them — and the little word that turns each verb on.
First you'll pick up seven routine and sequence words (la rutina, temprano, tarde, primero, antes, después, el despertador) so you can string actions into an order. Then you'll meet the reflexive pronoun — the tiny me, te, se, nos that rides in front of the verb. We're learning the pronoun on its own today, on a simple, regular verb (levantarse). The stem-changing reflexives wait until tomorrow — one new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to order my day with primero, antes, después and say temprano / tarde.
2
I will be able to attach the reflexive pronoun (me, te, se, nos) to a verb: me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, se levantan.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Early bird or night owl?

Some people are up with the sun; others come alive at night. Two words carry that idea in Spanish: temprano (early) and tarde (late). Notice your own rhythm before we name it.

🧠 PART 3 · EL PRONOMBRE REFLEXIVO 10 min

The little word that makes it yours

A reflexive verb has two parts: a pronoun that says who the action lands on, and the verb conjugated the normal way. Drop the -se, pick the matching pronoun, and put it in front. Here is levantarse (to get up), a nice regular -ar verb. Click each 🔊 and repeat.

SubjectPronounlevantarseListen & repeat
yomeme levanto Yo me levanto a las seis. (I get up at six.)
tútete levantas ¿A qué hora te levantas? (What time do you get up?)
usted / él / ellasese levantaElla se levanta tarde. (She gets up late.)
nosotros/asnosnos levantamos Nosotros nos duchamos por la mañana. (We shower in the morning.)
ustedes / ellos / ellassese levantan Ellos se acuestan tarde. (They go to bed late.)

(The verb endings are the same regular endings you already know — the only new piece is the pronoun in front. Notice usted/él/ella and ustedes/ellos/ellas both use se. In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting; vosotros, Spain's informal "you all," you'll meet later, labeled as European.)

Ellos se acuestan tarde. "They" → pronoun se + the verb form acuestan. (That verb has a small vowel change in the middle — that's tomorrow's story.)
🌎 CULTURA · EL RITMO DEL DÍA
La sobremesa

In Ecuador and across Latin America, the daily routine often includes something English has no word for: the sobremesa — the unhurried time spent at the table talking after a meal is finished. Lunch (el almuerzo) is frequently the biggest meal of the day, and families linger. It's a small reminder that a "routine" isn't only about rushing through tasks — it also makes room to sit down (sentarse) and be together.
