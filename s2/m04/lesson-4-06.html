★ Repaso y espiral — Retrieval Before the Quiz
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just pulling it back out of your head, which is where real learning happens.
This is your fourth meeting with reflexive verbs, and the first day of Week 2. Instead of new material, today you retrieve: an ear-quiz on the routine words, a short production prompt, and a spiral that reconnects reflexives to the stem-changing verbs of Módulo 2 (querer, dormir, empezar…) — the same e→ie and o→ue jumps that power me despierto and me acuesto. You'll also narrate a short routine using primero, antes, después. Bringing all of it together is exactly what the Day-10 quiz will ask of you.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize spoken routine verbs and match them to meaning.
2
I will be able to connect stem-changing reflexives to the Módulo 2 stem-changers and narrate a routine in order.

🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · EL CAMBIO DE RAÍZ 10 min

The same vowel jump, twice over

Your stem-changing reflexives aren't new magic — they reuse the exact jump you learned in Módulo 2. Listen across each row and hear the family resemblance:

Módulo 2 verb · listenSame changeMódulo 4 reflexive
quiero e→ie me despierto
empieza e→ie me siento
duerme o→ue me acuesto
pido e→i me visto

And remember the exception both families share: nosotros never jumps. Queremos, nos acostamos — back to the plain vowel.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does the vowel jump?
"Yo ___ a las diez." (I go to bed at ten.) — acostarse, o→ue.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · the nosotros trap
"Nosotros ___ temprano los lunes." (We wake up early on Mondays.)
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does the vowel jump?
"Ella ___ para la escuela." (She gets dressed for school.) — vestirse, e→i.
✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR · NARRA TU RUTINA 8 min

Tell your day in order, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — use the sequence words to put it in order — then reveal to check. This is a self-test, so being honest with yourself is where the value is. It's also a dress rehearsal for the Day-8 oral task.

1. Say: “First I wake up, and then I shower.”

Primero me despierto, y después me ducho. Sequence words primero / después; the e→ie jump in despierto.

2. Say: “Before school, I brush my teeth and get dressed.”

Antes de la escuela, me cepillo los dientes y me visto. Note los dientes (the teeth) — the pronoun me already says they're mine.

3. Say: “My family and I go to bed late on weekends.”

Mi familia y yo nos acostamos tarde los fines de semana. "My family and I" = nosotros → pronoun nos, and NO vowel jump: acostamos.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER OTRA VEZ 5 min

Camila returns — listen and read

You met this passage on Day 5. Press play, follow along, and this time notice how the sequence words carry you through his morning.

Play the reading

«Hola, me llamo Camila. Soy de Quito, Ecuador, la ciudad en la mitad del mundo. Entre semana me despierto temprano, a las seis, con el despertador. Primero me levanto y me ducho. Después me visto y me cepillo los dientes. Mi hermana se peina y se pone el uniforme. Desayunamos juntas y vamos a la escuela. Por la noche me acuesto tarde porque estudio mucho. Los fines de semana no me levanto temprano; ¡me quedo en la cama!»

🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
El uniforme escolar

Camila's sister se pone el uniforme — and so do most students across Ecuador and Latin America. School uniforms are the daily norm in both public and private schools, which is why ponerse el uniforme is a real step in a Latin-American teen's morning routine. It's a small cultural difference that changes what "getting dressed for school" actually means.

🌎 CULTURA A FONDO · ECUADOR
El dólar y el kichwa

Two everyday facts surprise many learners: since 2000, Ecuador's official currency has been the US dollar — so Camila pays for her breakfast in the same dollars used in the United States. And alongside Spanish, many Ecuadorians speak Kichwa, an Indigenous Andean language; words like ñaño (brother/buddy) slip into everyday Ecuadorian Spanish. A daily routine can be lived in more than one language.
