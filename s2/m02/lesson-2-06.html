★ Repaso y espiral — Which Verbs Change?
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just pulling it back out of your head, where real learning happens.
This is your fourth meeting with stem-changing verbs, and the first day of Week 2. Instead of new material, you retrieve: an ear-quiz on the home words, a short production round, and a spiral that lines up two families of verbs side by side — the regular verbs from Semester 1 (estudiar, comer, vivir) that keep their stem, and the new stem-changing verbs (querer, dormir) that don't. Knowing which is which is exactly what the Day-10 quiz asks.

I will be able to recognize spoken home vocabulary and match it to meaning.
2
I will be able to tell a regular verb (no change) from a stem-changing verb (boot change).

🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · ¿CAMBIA O NO CAMBIA? 10 min

Two families of verbs, side by side

In Semester 1 you learned regular verbs: the stem never changes, only the ending. This module's stem-changing verbs add one twist — the stem vowel shifts inside the boot. Listen across each row:

Verb (from)Stem?Example · listen
estudiar (S1, regular) no change Yo estudio español.
comer (S1, regular) no change Yo como con mi familia.
querer (M2, e→ie) changes Yo quiero una casa con jardín.
dormir (M2, o→ue) changes Mi hermano duerme mucho.

The trap to avoid: don't “fix” a regular verb that was never broken. Estudiar stays estudio — never “estuedio.” And boot verbs go back to normal for nosotros: queremos, dormimos, comemos, estudiamos.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does it change?
"Yo ___ español todos los días." (I study Spanish every day.) — estudiar is regular.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does it change?
"Yo ___ una casa grande." (I want a big house.) — querer is a boot verb.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · watch the nosotros
"Nosotros ___ ocho horas." (We sleep eight hours.) — dormir, but it's nosotros.
✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check the verb form and spelling.

1. Say: “I study at home, but I want to sleep.”

Yo estudio en casa, pero quiero dormir. Regular estudio (no change) + boot verb quiero (e→ie) + the infinitive dormir.

2. Say: “My sister cleans the bathroom and tidies her room.”

Mi hermana limpia el baño y ordena su habitación. Both limpiar and ordenar are regular — no stem change: limpia, ordena.

3. Say: “We want to tidy the living room.” (watch the nosotros!)

Nosotros queremos ordenar la sala. Nosotros → queremos keeps its stem (no change), then the infinitive ordenar.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER OTRA VEZ 5 min

Catalina returns — listen and read

Play the reading

«Me llamo Catalina y vivo en Santiago, Chile. Mi casa tiene una cocina grande, tres dormitorios y un jardín bonito. Todas las mañanas ordeno mi habitación y quiero ayudar en casa. Mi hermana prefiere dormir, pero después limpia la sala. Nosotros queremos mucho nuestra casa.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Sort the verbs out loud: vivo, ordeno, limpia are regular (no change); quiero, prefiere are boot verbs (change); and queremos is the nosotros that goes back to normal. Same verbs, two behaviors — that's the whole quiz.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Los quehaceres en familia

In many Chilean households, weekend mornings mean everyone pitching in: one person limpia the kitchen, another ordena the bedrooms, someone sweeps the patio. Chores are often framed as a shared family effort — nosotros ordenamos la casa juntos — rather than a solo task. Sharing the work is part of how a household runs.

🏔 CULTURA A FONDO · CHILE
El español chileno

Chilean Spanish is famous across Latin America for being fast and full of local slang. A classic tag word is ¿cachai? (“you get it?”), and Chileans often add -po to sí and no (sípo, no po). The grammar you're learning is standard everywhere — but if you ever chat with a Chilean friend, expect a lively, playful accent all its own.
