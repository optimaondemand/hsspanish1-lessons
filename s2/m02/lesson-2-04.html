Lesson 2.04
★ Aplicación — Using the Boot Verbs
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned the rule. Today you use it — in writing and by ear.
This is your second encounter with stem-changing verbs, one day after the first — the spacing is on purpose. There's no new grammar today. You'll choose the right form yourself in a short guided writing, then train your ear with a six-sentence dictation using the very verbs from yesterday. It's all ungraded practice: mistakes here are exactly how the habit gets built.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to write the correct stem-changing form and explain whether it's a boot form or nosotros.
2
I will be able to listen to a spoken sentence with a boot verb and write down what I hear.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

The rule, in one breath

Before you write anything, say it out loud: the stem vowel changes in every form except nosotros. Inside the boot → change (quiero, puedes, duerme, juega, pido). Outside the boot (nosotros) → no change (queremos, podemos, dormimos).

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
The nosotros exception — how solid does it feel right now?
✍️ PART 2 · ESCRITURA GUIADA 14 min

You make the choice

On paper or in a document, copy each sentence and fill the blank with the correct present-tense form of the verb in parentheses. Say why to yourself — boot form or nosotros? Commit to all six before you reveal the key.

INSIDE THE BOOT — changes

yo · tú · él/ella/usted · ellos/ellas/ustedes
quiero · puedes · duerme · juegan

OUTSIDE — no change

nosotros / nosotras only
queremos · podemos · dormimos · jugamos
  1. Yo ______ (querer) una casa con jardín.
  2. Nosotros ______ (poder) limpiar el baño.
  3. Mi hermana ______ (dormir) en el dormitorio de arriba.
  4. Los niños ______ (jugar) en el jardín.
  5. El día ______ (empezar) en la cocina.
  6. Yo ______ (pedir) ayuda con los quehaceres.
1. quiero — boot form (e→ie). (Yo quiero una casa con jardín.)
2. podemos — nosotros, outside the boot → no change. (Not “puedemos.”)
3. duerme — boot form (o→ue), él/ella.
4. juegan — boot form (u→ue), ellos.
5. empieza — boot form (e→ie), the day “begins.”
6. pido — boot form (e→i), yo.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Write two more sentences about your real home life today — one with a boot form (yo quiero…, mi hermano duerme…) and one with nosotros (nosotros podemos…). Read them aloud and check the vowel each time.
🎧 PART 3 · DICTADO 13 min

Listen and write — six sentences

These are the boot-verb sentences from yesterday — now the training wheels are off. For each one: click 🔊 (play it as many times as you need), write exactly what you hear, mind the accent marks, then reveal to check. Don't reveal until you've written your best guess.

1
Write what you hear…
Yo quiero una casa con jardín. (I want a house with a garden.) — boot form quiero (e→ie).
2
Write what you hear…
¿Puedes limpiar la cocina? (Can you clean the kitchen?) — note the opening ¿ and boot form puedes (o→ue).
3
Write what you hear…
Mi hermano duerme mucho. (My brother sleeps a lot.) — boot form duerme (o→ue).
4
Write what you hear…
El día empieza en la cocina. (The day begins in the kitchen.) — boot form empieza (e→ie).
5
Write what you hear…
Ella juega en el jardín. (She plays in the garden.) — boot form juega (u→ue).
6
Write what you hear…
Nosotros queremos ordenar la sala. (We want to tidy the living room.) — nosotros → queremos, NO change. This is the one to catch.

Five of these changed their stem — but number 6 (nosotros) did not. Training your ear to expect the exception is exactly the point.

🧠 PART 4 · DOS PARA TERMINAR 8 min

Two to finish on

Same question every time: is this a boot form (changes) or nosotros (no change)?

✅ TRY IT · choose the right form
"Mis padres ______ ordenar la casa los sábados." (My parents want to tidy the house on Saturdays.) — querer.
✅ TRY IT · choose the right form
"Mi amiga y yo ______ en el parque." (My friend and I play in the park.) — jugar.

One to build yourself: how would you say "I sleep downstairs"? Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Yo duermo abajo. Boot form duermo (o→ue) + abajo (downstairs).
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
La once chilena

Around five or six in the evening, many Chilean families gather for la once — an afternoon “tea” with bread (pan), avocado, cheese, ham, and tea or coffee. It's a relaxed moment at the kitchen or dining table, often more social than dinner. Nosotros queremos tomar once juntos — “we want to have once together” — and notice: queremos, the nosotros form, keeps its stem.
