★ La casa de Catalina — A Home in Santiago
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Your third encounter with the boot verbs — this time inside a real story about a home in Chile.
Today you read (and hear) a short passage: Catalina describes her house in Santiago, Chile — the rooms, the chores she does, and what everyone in the family wants and prefers. The vocabulary and the stem-changing verbs you've practiced all week are woven right in. Then a culture spotlight takes you deeper into Chile.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to understand a short paragraph describing a home and its chores.
2
I will be able to find the stem-changing verbs in a text and explain each one.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Before you read

You're about to meet Catalina, a student in Santiago. As you read, you'll recognize almost everything: rooms, furniture, chores, and boot verbs like quiere and prefiere. First, a quick thought about your own home.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
In your home, who does most of the tidying up?
📖 PART 2 · A LEER 12 min

Listen and read along

Press play, follow the words, then read it again on your own. The stem-changing verbs are in bold.

Play the reading

«Me llamo Catalina y vivo en Santiago, Chile. Mi casa tiene una cocina grande, tres dormitorios y un jardín bonito. Todas las mañanas ordeno mi habitación y quiero ayudar en casa. Mi hermana prefiere dormir, pero después limpia la sala. Nosotros queremos mucho nuestra casa.»

Glossary: tiene = has · grande = big · bonito = pretty · ayudar = to help · después = afterward · nuestra casa = our house. Note: querer a place/person can also mean “to love” it — queremos mucho nuestra casa = we love our house.

🧠 PART 4 · CAZA DE VERBOS 6 min

Find the boot verbs

Go back to the passage and point to each stem-changing verb. Say its type out loud, then reveal to check.

quiero — querer, e→ie (yo, boot form).
prefiere — preferir, e→ie (él/ella, boot form).
queremos — querer, nosotros → no change (outside the boot). This is the one to notice: it keeps the e.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Write your own three-sentence “Mi casa” paragraph, copying Catalina's shape: where you live, what your house has, and one boot verb about a chore (Yo quiero… / prefiero…). Read it aloud.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Un país largo y delgado

Chile is one of the longest, narrowest countries on Earth — over 4,000 km from the Atacama Desert in the north to Patagonia in the south, but rarely more than 200 km wide, pressed between the Andes and the Pacific. That shape gives Chile almost every climate: desert, Mediterranean valleys around Santiago, forests, and glaciers. A home in Santiago and a home in the far south can feel like different worlds — yet families everywhere share the same rhythm of ordenar la casa and gathering for la once.

🏔 CULTURA A FONDO · CHILE
Valparaíso: casas de colores en los cerros

A short trip from Santiago, the port city of Valparaíso spills up steep hills (cerros) in a riot of brightly painted houses, murals, and old funicular elevators (ascensores) that carry people up and down the slopes. UNESCO named its historic quarter a World Heritage Site. Here la casa becomes public art — every wall a canvas — and Neruda chose one of these hills for La Sebastiana, one of his three beloved homes.
