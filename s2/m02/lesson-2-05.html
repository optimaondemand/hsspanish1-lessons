Glossary: tiene = has · grande = big · bonito = pretty · todas las mañanas = every morning · ayudar = to help · prefiere = prefers (preferir, e→ie) · después = afterward · nuestra casa = our house. VESPIRAL · ¿CAMBIA O NO CAMBIA?10 min

Two families of verbs, side by side

In Semester 1 you learned regular verbs: the stem never changes, only the ending. This module's stem-changing verbs add one twist — the stem vowel shifts inside the boot. Listen across each row:

Verb (from)Stem?Example · listen
estudiar (S1, regular) no change Yo estudio español.
comer (S1, regular) no change Yo como con mi familia.
querer (M2, e→ie) changes Yo quiero una casa con jardín.
dormir (M2, o→ue) changes Mi hermano duerme mucho.

The trap to avoid: don't “fix” a regular verb that was never broken. Estudiar stays estudio — never “estuedio.” And boot verbs go back to normal for nosotros: queremos, dormimos, comemos, estudiamos.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does it change?
"Yo ___ español todos los días." (I study Spanish every day.) — estudiar is regular.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · does it change?
"Yo ___ una casa grande." (I want a big house.) — querer is a boot verb.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · watch the nosotros
"Nosotros ___ ocho horas." (We sleep eight hours.) — dormir, but it's nosotros.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Un país largo y delgado

Chile is one of the longest, narrowest countries on Earth — over 4,000 km from the Atacama Desert in the north to Patagonia in the south, but rarely more than 200 km wide, pressed between the Andes and the Pacific. That shape gives Chile almost every climate: desert, Mediterranean valleys around Santiago, forests, and glaciers. A home in Santiago and a home in the far south can feel like different worlds — yet families everywhere share the same rhythm of ordenar la casa and gathering for la once.

🏔 CULTURA A FONDO · CHILE
Valparaíso: casas de colores en los cerros

A short trip from Santiago, the port city of Valparaíso spills up steep hills (cerros) in a riot of brightly painted houses, murals, and old funicular elevators (ascensores) that carry people up and down the slopes. UNESCO named its historic quarter a World Heritage Site. Here la casa becomes public art — every wall a canvas — and Neruda chose one of these hills for La Sebastiana, one of his three beloved homes.
