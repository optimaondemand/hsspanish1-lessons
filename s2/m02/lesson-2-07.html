IPARA EMPEZAR5 min

Why these sounds matter

The stem change isn't just spelling — it's a sound. When e becomes ie and o becomes ue, two vowels glide together into one smooth syllable. Say it clean and your boot verbs instantly sound more native. Today we rehearse that glide, then record.

IILOS DIPTONGOS IE / UE11 min

Two vowels, one smooth syllable

A diptongo (diphthong) is two vowels blended into a single glide. In the boot verbs, the stem change creates one: quiero = “kye-ro”, puedo = “pwe-do.” Don't say the two vowels separately (“ee-eh”) — slide through them. Click 🔊 and echo each one:

  • quieroie glide → “KYE-ro”
  • puedesue glide → “PWE-des”
  • duermeue glide → “DWER-me”
  • empiezaie glide → “em-PYE-sa”

Notice: the nosotros forms (queremos, dormimos) have no diphthong — because the stem doesn't change, there's no glide to make.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say each pair back to back to feel the difference: queremos → quiero, podemos → puedo, dormimos → duermo. Recording yourself on your phone and playing it back is the fastest way to hear whether your glide is smooth.
IIILA JOTA (J)9 min

A breath from the back of the throat

The Spanish j (la jota) is nothing like the English “j” in jam. It's a soft, breathy h-sound made at the back of the throat — like clearing it gently. You already use it in two module words:

  • el jardín“har-DEEN” — breathy j
  • juega“HWE-ga” — j + the ue glide together

The letter g before e or i makes the same throat sound (inteligente) — but before a, o, u it's a hard “g” (juega, amigo).

IVLAS DOS “C”5 min

One letter, two sounds

The word cocina shows both sounds of c in one word. Before a, o, u the c is a hard “k”; before e, i it softens to “s” (in Latin America).

  • la cocina“ko” (hard) + “see” (soft) → ko-SEE-na

How would you say the c in cama (bed) — hard or soft? Decide, then reveal.

Hard “k” — c before a is always hard: “KA-ma.” The c only softens before e or i (as in co-ci-na).
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Hablar rápido, hablar claro (Speaking fast, speaking clearly)

Chileans are famous for speaking quickly and dropping the final s in casual speech (¿cómo estái? for ¿cómo estás?). As a learner you should do the opposite — speak a little slowly and pronounce every sound. Clear, unhurried Spanish is understood everywhere, and it's exactly what earns full marks on today's recording.

🏔 CULTURA A FONDO · CHILE
Gabriela Mistral, la voz del sur (Gabriela Mistral, the voice of the South)

Chile has produced two Nobel Prize-winning poets. The first was Gabriela Mistral, a rural schoolteacher from the Elqui Valley who in 1945 became the first Latin American woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. Her poems about children, home, and the land are read across the Spanish-speaking world — a reminder that a clear, honest voice, carefully used, can reach very far. Speak yours with care today.

VTAREA ORAL10 min

Your graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself describing your home and the chores you do. Say what rooms your home has, and use at least two stem-changing verbs (for example querer, poder, preferir, jugar) to say what you and your family want, can, or prefer to do. Aim for 30–45 seconds.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. En mi casa hay una cocina, una sala y dos dormitorios. Todos los días yo quiero ordenar mi habitación. Los sábados nosotros limpiamos juntos, y yo prefiero limpiar la cocina.»
(That's a home description plus two boot verbs — quiero and prefiero — and a nosotros chore.)

Need to hear the target verbs once more before recording? Replay these models:

  • Yo quiero…querer — e→ie
  • ¿Puedes…?poder — o→ue
  • Ella juega…jugar — u→ue
