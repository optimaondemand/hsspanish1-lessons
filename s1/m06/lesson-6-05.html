★ A leer — Valentina y Colombia
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just reading. You'll meet Valentina and hear the -er / -ir verbs at work in a real little story.
This is your third encounter with these verbs — and the most relaxed. Instead of conjugating in a drill, you'll simply notice the verbs at work inside meaningful input: a short paragraph about a teenager in Medellín and what she does for fun. Read it, listen to it, and let the pattern settle. Then we'll take a cultural detour to Colombia, where Valentina lives.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and understand a short paragraph full of -er / -ir verbs.
2
I will be able to locate Colombia and name one thing it is known for.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 8 min

Before you read

Good readers guess before they read. Valentina is a teenager who lives in Medellín, Colombia. As you go into the paragraph, keep one question in mind: which verbs tell you what she does for fun — and which of them are -er and which are -ir?

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which free-time activity sounds most like your weekend?
📚 PART 2 · LA LECTURA · VALENTINA 16 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud by a native speaker. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know the important ones.

Escuchar la lectura completa — Valentina

«Hola, me llamo Valentina. Vivo en Medellín, Colombia. En mi tiempo libre, corro en el parque y leo muchos libros. Los fines de semana, mi familia y yo comemos juntos. Por la tarde, escribo en mi diario y aprendo a bailar salsa. Comparto mi tiempo con mis amigos, y siempre asistimos a las fiestas del barrio.»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

me llamo = my name is · vivo = I live · tiempo libre = free time · corro = I run · leo = I read · muchos libros = many books · fines de semana = weekends · comemos = we eat · juntos = together · por la tarde = in the afternoon · escribo = I write · mi diario = my diary · aprendo a bailar = I'm learning to dance · comparto = I share · siempre = always · asistimos a = we attend · las fiestas del barrio = the neighborhood parties
✅ PART 3 · ¿COMPRENDISTE? 16 min

Check your understanding

Answer from the paragraph. These are reading questions — you're showing you understood.

✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Dónde vive Valentina? (Where does Valentina live?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Qué hace Valentina en el parque? (What does she do at the park?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Qué aprende Valentina? (What is she learning?)

Detective question: the paragraph has two nosotros verbs — comemos and asistimos. Why does one end in -emos and the other in -imos? Say it out loud, then reveal.

comemos is from comer (an -er verb) → -emos. asistimos is from asistir (an -ir verb) → -imos. Same subject, two families — the one difference, live in a real sentence.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go back and be a detective. Find every verb and label its family: vivo, escribo, comparto, asistimos are -ir; corro, leo, comemos, aprendo are -er. For each, whisper the subject and ending. This is the same skill the Day 10 quiz will ask for — but here it's just noticing.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
Medellín y la vida al aire libre

Valentina is from Medellín, Colombia's second-largest city, nicknamed «la ciudad de la eterna primavera» (the city of eternal spring) for its year-round mild weather. Its capital is Bogotá, and Colombia sits at the northern tip of South America, touching both the Caribbean and the Pacific.

Colombians are famous for how they vivir their free time together — salsa and cumbia fill weekend fiestas, and on Sundays the Ciclovía turns city streets into open space for running and cycling. The country is also the birthplace of writer Gabriel García Márquez, a Nobel laureate whose books people around the world leen to this day.

★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Bartolomé de las Casas (1484–1566) was a Spanish friar who, after witnessing the abuse of Indigenous peoples in the Americas, spent his life telling that hard truth — writing and arguing before kings that these communities deserved dignity and protection. Speaking an uncomfortable truth to the powerful is honesty at its bravest. Él escribe la verdad y comparte su voz — two of this module's own verbs, doing the work of an honest life.
