★ Fonética — Sounding Like Yourself in Spanish
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written the -er / -ir verbs all week. Today you say them — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic. Spanish sounds are more regular than English, and three habits carry most of a good accent: pure, short vowels; predictable stress; and the sound of the ñ. We'll rehearse each one out loud using words you already know, then you'll record your first graded Tarea oral — a few true sentences about what you do in your free time and on weekends.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why pronunciation is the easy part

Here's good news: Spanish spelling almost always tells you exactly how to say a word. There are no silent letters lurking the way English has them in knight or through. If you learn a few sound rules, you can read a brand-new word aloud and be right. Today we lock in those rules — and then you use them on air.

📚 PART 2 · LAS CINCO VOCALES 11 min

Five vowels, one clean sound each

English vowels slide and stretch (say "no" slowly and you'll hear it drift to "noh-oo"). Spanish vowels do not glide — each is short, crisp, and always the same. Keep them clipped.

VowelSay it like…Keep it…
athe a in "father"short, open
ethe e in "bet"short, no glide to "ay"
ithe ee in "see" (but quick)tight, brief
othe o in "for"round, no glide to "ow"
uthe oo in "food" (but quick)short, lips forward

Model words — listen, then echo

These are all words you already know. Click 🔊, then say each one back, clipping every vowel. Listen for how steady each vowel stays.

  • el parqueclean a and e, no glide
  • la películafour crisp vowels: e-í-u-a
  • correrround o, short e, rolled rr
  • escribirshort e, two tight i's
  • el tiempo libreclean ie and e, no drift
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say the vowel chain out loud, slowly, five times: a – e – i – o – u. Then replay película and match all four of its vowels exactly. Recording yourself on your phone and playing it back is the single fastest way to hear your own glides.
🧠 PART 3 · EL ACENTO (STRESS) 9 min

Which syllable do I punch?

Spanish stress follows two simple defaults — and a written accent mark (tilde) overrides them:

  • Word ends in a vowel, -n, or -s → stress the second-to-last syllable. co-RRE-mos, es-cri-BI-mos, VI-ven.
  • Word ends in any other consonant → stress the last syllable. co-MER, vi-VIR, es-cri-BIR.
  • A written accent beats both rules — just stress the marked vowel. pe-LÍ-cu-la.

This is why the infinitives punch the end — comER, vivIR — but the conjugated forms fall back to the default. Listen for the shift:

  • Nosotros vivimos…default → vi-VI-mos
  • Ellos viven…ends in -n → VI-ven
  • Yo como…ends in vowel → CO-mo

The word comemos ends in -s. Say it out loud and decide which syllable you punch — then reveal.

Stress the second-to-last syllable: co-ME-mos. It ends in -s, so the default rule applies — no accent mark needed.

Say the infinitive escribir out loud — where does the stress go, and why? Decide, then reveal.

es-cri-BIR — it ends in -r (a consonant that isn't n or s), so the default lands the stress on the last syllable. No accent mark needed.
🔈 PART 4 · LA Ñ 5 min

The one letter English doesn't have

The ñ is its own letter in the Spanish alphabet, and it makes the ny sound you already say in English "canyon" or "onion." Try these out loud:

  • español (Spanish) → es-pa-NYOL
  • mañana (tomorrow / morning) → ma-NYA-na
  • compañero (companion, classmate) → com-pa-NYE-ro

You've already met the ñ in the friendly sign-off at the end of these lessons: ¡hasta mañana! Now you know it's a whole letter with a sound of its own.

🌎 CULTURA · LA Ñ
Una letra con historia

Medieval scribes writing Latin doubled the n in words like annus (year); to save costly parchment they began writing a small n above the first — that little wave became the tilde, and ñ was born. Today the letter is a proud symbol of the Spanish language itself: it appears in España, in the logo of the Instituto Cervantes, and in the very word español.

★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Your recording asks for true sentences about your own free time — and telling the truth about yourself, even in imperfect Spanish, is its own small act of honesty. Don't invent a fancier life for the mic; say what you really do. A genuine, plainly-said sentence is worth more than a polished lie. ¡Sé honesto/a y habla con confianza!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your first graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself describing what you do in your free time and on weekends, in at least three true sentences. Use -er / -ir verbs (like como, leo, corro, vivo, escribo, comparto) and try one sentence with me gusta + an infinitive. Aim for 20–40 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. En mi tiempo libre, corro en el parque y leo muchos libros. Los fines de semana, como con mi familia y escribo en mi diario. Me gusta compartir el tiempo con mis amigos.»
(That's several -er / -ir verbs — what I do — plus me gusta + an infinitive.)

Need help hearing the target verbs one more time before you record? Replay these models:

  • Yo como con mi familia.comer — an -er verb
  • Yo vivo en Colombia.vivir — an -ir verb
  • Yo leo y escribo en español.both families, side by side
