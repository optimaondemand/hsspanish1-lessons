Lesson 6.02
★ Verbos -er / -ir — A New Family of Actions
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the fun. Today you get the verbs to do it — a whole new family.
In Módulo 2 you learned -ar verbs (like estudiar, hablar). Spanish has two more families: verbs ending in -er (like comer, to eat) and -ir (like vivir, to live). First you'll pick up a set of these verbs, then you'll meet their present-tense endings. We're learning the forms on their own today; the neat comparison of all three families waits for tomorrow. One new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and say common -er and -ir verbs.
2
I will be able to use the present-tense forms of comer (-er) and vivir (-ir).

Actions you already do every day

You already eat, read, run, and live somewhere every single day. By the end of this lesson you'll have Spanish verbs for all of it. Start by noticing what you actually do in a normal afternoon.

📚 PART 2 · LOS VERBOS -ER / -IR 12 min

Listen, then repeat

These are all in the infinitive (the dictionary form). Click the 🔊 and say each back. Watch the ending: -er or -ir — that's the family the verb belongs to.

Verbos -ER

  • comerto eat
  • beberto drink
  • leerto read
  • correrto run
  • aprenderto learn
  • comprenderto understand

Verbos -IR

  • vivirto live
  • escribirto write
  • abrirto open

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go through the list again and picture a real moment for each verb: what do you comer for lunch? what do you leer for fun? who do you compartir with? Attaching a verb to a real moment is one of the fastest ways to make it stick.
🧠 PART 3 · LAS TERMINACIONES 10 min

Meet the endings — comer and vivir

A verb changes its ending to match who is doing it — you saw this with -ar verbs in Módulo 2. To conjugate, drop the -er or -ir and add the ending. Click the 🔊 to hear each example, then repeat it aloud.

comer (to eat) — an -ER verb

SubjectcomerListen & repeat
yocomo Yo como con mi familia. (I eat with my family.)
túcomes ¿Qué comes tú? (What do you eat?)
usted / él / ellacome Ella come fruta. (She eats fruit.)
nosotros/ascomemosNosotros comemos juntos. (We eat together.)
ustedes / ellos / ellascomenEllos comen en la fiesta. (They eat at the party.)

vivir (to live) — an -IR verb

SubjectvivirListen & repeat
yovivo Yo vivo en Colombia. (I live in Colombia.)
túvivesTú vives aquí. (You live here.)
usted / él / ellaviveÉl vive en Bogotá. (He lives in Bogotá.)
nosotros/asvivimos Nosotros vivimos en la ciudad. (We live in the city.)
ustedes / ellos / ellasviven Ellos viven en Bogotá. (They live in Bogotá.)

(In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting. Vosotros, Spain's informal "you all," you'll meet later, labeled as European. Tiny spoiler for tomorrow: look at comemos vs. vivimos — that nosotros form is the one place where -er and -ir differ.)

🌎 CULTURA · TRES FAMILIAS DE VERBOS
-ar, -er, -ir

Every regular Spanish verb belongs to one of three families, named for the last two letters of the infinitive: -ar (hablar), -er (comer), and -ir (vivir). Learn one verb from each family well and you can conjugate hundreds more — the endings repeat. You now own two of the three; tomorrow you'll line all three up side by side.
