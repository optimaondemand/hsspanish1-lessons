Lesson 3.08
★ Fonética — Sounding Like Yourself in Spanish
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written ser and estar all week. Today you say them — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic. Spanish sounds are more regular than English, and three habits carry most of a good accent: pure, short vowels; predictable stress; and the sound of the ñ. We'll rehearse each one out loud using words you already know, then you'll record your first graded Tarea oral — three true sentences about where you are and how you feel.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pronounce the five pure Spanish vowels, place word stress correctly, and say the ñ.
2
I will be able to record three true sentences (one ser, two estar) describing where I am and how I feel.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why pronunciation is the easy part

Here's good news: Spanish spelling almost always tells you exactly how to say a word. There are no silent letters lurking the way English has them in knight or through. If you learn a few sound rules, you can read a brand-new word aloud and be right. Today we lock in those rules — and then you use them on air.

📚 PART 2 · LAS CINCO VOCALES 11 min

Five vowels, one clean sound each

English vowels slide and stretch (say "no" slowly and you'll hear it drift to "noh-oo"). Spanish vowels do not glide — each is short, crisp, and always the same. Keep them clipped.

VowelSay it like…Keep it…
athe a in "father"short, open
ethe e in "bet"short, no glide to "ay"
ithe ee in "see" (but quick)tight, brief
othe o in "for"round, no glide to "ow"
uthe oo in "food" (but quick)short, lips forward

Model words — listen, then echo

These are all words you already know. Click 🔊, then say each one back, clipping every vowel. Listen for how steady each vowel stays.

  • la casathe two a's: identical, short
  • la escuelaclean e and ue, no glide
  • la ciudadtight i, forward u
  • ocupadoround o, forward u, open a
  • aquíopen a + tight stressed í
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say the vowel chain out loud, slowly, five times: a – e – i – o – u. Then replay ocupado and see if you can match all four of its vowels exactly. Recording yourself on your phone and playing it back is the single fastest way to hear your own glides.
🧠 PART 3 · EL ACENTO (STRESS) 9 min

Which syllable do I punch?

Spanish stress follows two simple defaults — and a written accent mark (tilde) overrides them:

  • Word ends in a vowel, -n, or -s → stress the second-to-last syllable. ca-SA, con-TEN-to, es-TA-mos.
  • Word ends in any other consonant → stress the last syllable. ciu-DAD, tra-ba-JAR.
  • A written accent beats both rules — just stress the marked vowel. a-QUÍ, es-TÁ, es-TÁS, es-TÁN.

This is why estar carries so many tildes: without the mark, "estas" would be stressed ES-tas. The accent pulls the stress to the end — es-TÁS. Listen for that punch:

  • ¿Cómo estás?stress lands on -tÁS
  • Ellos están ocupados.-tÁN punched; ocupados on -PA-
  • Nosotros estamos en la escuela.no tilde → default es-TA-mos

The word contento ends in a vowel. Say it out loud and decide which syllable you punch — then reveal.

Stress the second-to-last syllable: con-TEN-to. It ends in a vowel, so the default rule applies — no accent mark needed.

Say la ciudad out loud — where does the stress go, and why? Decide, then reveal.

ciu-DAD — it ends in -d (a consonant that isn't n or s), so the default lands the stress on the last syllable. No accent mark needed.
🔈 PART 4 · LA Ñ 5 min

The one letter English doesn't have

The ñ is its own letter in the Spanish alphabet, and it makes the ny sound you already say in English "canyon" or "onion." Try these out loud:

  • mañana (tomorrow / morning) → ma-NYA-na
  • español (Spanish) → es-pa-NYOL
  • niño (child) → NI-nyo

You've already met the ñ in the friendly sign-off at the end of these lessons: ¡hasta mañana! Now you know it's a whole letter with a sound of its own.

🌎 CULTURA · LA Ñ
Una letra con historia

Medieval scribes writing Latin doubled the n in words like annus (year); to save costly parchment they began writing a small n above the first — that little wave became the tilde, and ñ was born. Today the letter is a proud symbol of the Spanish language itself: it appears in España, in the name of the Instituto Cervantes' logo, and in the very word español.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Speaking a new language out loud — and recording it — takes courage. Everyone's first recordings feel awkward; that feeling is not a sign you're doing it wrong, it's a sign you're doing it at all. Rehearse, breathe, and press record. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your first graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself saying three true sentences about yourself right now: one with ser and two with estar. Together they should say who you are, where you are, and how you feel. Aim for 20–40 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. Soy Daniel y soy de Florida. Ahora estoy en casa. Hoy estoy un poco cansado, pero contento.»
(That's one ser idea — who/where I'm from — and two estar ideas — where I am, how I feel.)

Need help hearing the target verbs one more time before you record? Replay these models:

  • Yo soy de Cuba.ser — identity / origin
  • Yo estoy en casa.estar — location
  • Mi hermano está en el trabajo, pero es profesor.both, side by side

💡 CONSEJO · plan before you record

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.
