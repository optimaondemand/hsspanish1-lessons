Lesson 3.05
★ A leer — Sofía y el Caribe
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just reading. You'll meet Sofía again and hear ser and estar working together in a real little story.
This is your third encounter with the ser/estar contrast — and the most relaxed. Instead of choosing verbs in a drill, you'll simply notice them at work inside meaningful input: a short paragraph about a girl from Cuba now living in Miami. Read it, listen to it, and let the pattern settle. Then we'll take a quick cultural detour to the Caribbean, where Sofía is from.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and understand a short paragraph that uses ser and estar together.
2
I will be able to locate Cuba and the Dominican Republic and name one thing each is known for.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 8 min

Before you read

Good readers guess before they read. Sofía is a teenager who moved from La Habana (Havana), Cuba, to Miami with her family. As you go into the paragraph, keep one question in mind: which details are about who she is (identity → ser), and which are about where she is or how she feels today (→ estar)?

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think of a place your family is from and a place you are right now. Which pair sounds most like you?
📚 PART 2 · LA LECTURA · SOFÍA 16 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud by a native speaker. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know the important ones.

Escuchar la lectura completa — Sofía

«Hola, me llamo Sofía. Soy de La Habana, Cuba. Ahora estoy en Miami con mi familia. Hoy estoy muy contenta porque no hay clases. Mi hermano está cansado, pero mi madre está ocupada en casa. Nosotros somos cubanos, y siempre estamos juntos.»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

me llamo = my name is · ahora = now · hoy = today · muy = very · porque = because · no hay clases = there's no school · mi hermano = my brother · mi madre = my mother · en casa = at home · cubanos = Cuban · siempre = always · juntos = together
✅ PART 3 · ¿COMPRENDISTE? 16 min

Check your understanding

Answer from the paragraph. These are reading questions — you're showing you understood, not choosing a verb.

✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿De dónde es Sofía? (Where is Sofía from?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Cómo está Sofía hoy? (How does Sofía feel today?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Quién está cansado? (Who is tired?)

In your own words: why does Sofía say somos cubanos but estamos juntos? Say it out loud, then reveal.

Somos cubanos is who they are — a lasting identity, so ser. Estamos juntos is their situation right now — together today, so estar. Same family, two different kinds of "to be."
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go back to the paragraph and be a detective. Find every form of ser (there are two: soy, somos) and every form of estar (there are five: estoy, estoy, está, está, estamos). For each one, whisper why: identity/origin → ser, or location/feeling → estar? This is the same skill the Day 10 quiz will ask for — but here it's just noticing.
🌎 CULTURA · EL CARIBE
Cuba y la República Dominicana

Sofía is from the Caribbean — a region of warm islands where Spanish is spoken with its own bright, fast music. La Habana es la capital de Cuba y está en la costa (on the coast) — one more perfect ser/estar pair: es tells you what Havana is, está tells you where it is.

Cuba gave the world son — the guitar-and-percussion sound that later grew into salsa. Next door, the Dominican Republic (whose capital is Santo Domingo) gave us bachata, romantic guitar music now heard everywhere. On both islands, Spanish blends with African and Taíno roots — you can hear that history in every song.

★ LA VIRTUD · RESPONSIBILITY

Notice how Sofía's family shares the load: her mother está ocupada en casa, and the family siempre está juntos. Taking care of a home together — each person carrying part of the day's work — is quiet, everyday responsibility. A family, like a sentence, holds together when each part does its job.
