📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've drilled ser in isolation. Today you meet it inside a real introduction — and visit the country behind it.
This is your third encounter with ser, and the first time you'll hear it read aloud in a connected passage. You'll listen to Daniela introduce himself, follow along, and answer a few comprehension questions — then take a short cultural trip to México, our featured country for this module. No grading today; this is about hearing the language do its job in the wild.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Before you read

You're about to hear a student named Daniela introduce himself — the same kind of introduction you'll record yourself in a few days. Listen for the two things every introduction includes: who and where from.

📖 PART 2 · LA LECTURA DE DIEGO 15 min

Listen and read

Press play, follow along, and notice each form of ser. Replay as many times as you like.

Play the reading

«Hola, me llamo Daniela. Soy de la Ciudad de México y soy estudiante. Mi amiga Ana es cubana; ella es muy simpática e inteligente. Nosotras somos amigas. Yo soy alta y trabajadora. ¿Y tú? ¿De dónde eres?»

✅ COMPRENSIÓN · where is Daniela from?
Daniela says «Soy de la Ciudad de México.» Where is she from?
✅ COMPRENSIÓN · what is Ana like?
How does Daniela describe her friend Ana?
✅ COMPRENSIÓN · the form of ser
In «Nosotros ___ amigos», which form of ser does Daniela use?
⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Point to each form of ser in the passage and say its job out loud: soy (who Daniela is / where she's from), es (what Ana is like), somos (identity of the pair), eres (her question to you). Then answer her: ¡Hola, Daniela! Yo soy de…
🇲🇽 CULTURA · MÉXICO
El país de Daniela

México is the most populous Spanish-speaking country in the world — more than 120 million people. Its capital, la Ciudad de México (CDMX), is one of the largest cities on Earth, built on the site of the ancient Aztec capital Tenochtitlán. From tacos and mariachi to Frida Kahlo and the ruins of Teotihuacán, Mexican culture reaches deeply into the United States and the wider world.

One language note: México sits in North América — and in Spanish, América means the whole continent. That's exactly why Spanish uses estadounidense for someone specifically from the Estados Unidos: everyone in the Americas is, in a sense, americano.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Daniela ends by turning the question to you: ¿De dónde eres? Answering out loud — introducing yourself in a language you're still learning — takes a real, everyday kind of courage. It's the same courage that moved Simón Bolívar to imagine free nations where others saw only empire. You won't liberate a continent today, but saying «Yo soy…» in Spanish is its own brave first step.
