★ ¿Cómo eres? — Describing People & the Verb ser
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned who and where from. Today you learn how to describe a person — and the verb that carries it all.
First you'll pick up a set of describing words (smart, kind, tall…). Then you'll meet the subject pronouns (yo, tú, él…) and the five forms of ser, the verb of who you are and where you're from. We're learning the forms on their own today — just the shapes, riding on words you already know. The famous tú vs. usted choice, and description in full, wait until tomorrow; one new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

I will be able to describe a person with common adjectives (inteligente, simpático, alto…).
2

💡 A QUICK NOTE ON ENDINGS (just notice it for now)

Many describing words add -o/-a to match the person, like the nationalities from Day 1: alto / alta, simpático / simpática. A few are the same for boys and girls — inteligente, amable, grande, joven don't change. And one type just adds -a: trabajador → trabajadora.

You don't need to master this today — just notice it. The full adjective system, with word order, comes in Módulo 7.

🧠 PART 3 · LOS PRONOMBRES Y EL VERBO SER 11 min

Meet ser — "to be" for who and where from

A verb changes its ending to match who is doing it — and Spanish often skips the pronoun because the ending already tells you. Here are the subject pronouns paired with the five forms of ser. Click the 🔊 in a row to hear the example, then repeat it aloud.

Subject pronounserListen & repeat
yosoy Yo soy de México. (I'm from Mexico.)
túeres ¿De dónde eres? (Where are you from?)
usted / él / ellaes Ella es simpática. (She is nice.)
nosotros/assomos Nosotros somos estudiantes. (We are students.)
ustedes / ellos / ellasson Ellos son mexicanos. (They are Mexican.)

(In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting — formal or casual. Vosotros, Spain's informal "you all," you'll meet later, labeled as European. The yo form is worth memorizing on its own: soy.)

Your turn: how would you say "She is intelligent"? Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Ella es inteligente. "She" → es; and inteligente is one of the words that stays the same for a boy or a girl.
🌎 CULTURA · EL NOMBRE COMPLETO
Two last names, not one

In most of the Spanish-speaking world, a full nombre carries two surnames — first the father's, then the mother's. So the writer Gabriel García Márquez is García (father) Márquez (mother). Your identity, in Spanish, quietly honors both sides of your family — a small window into how much la familia matters, a theme you'll return to in Módulo 4.
