★ Ser en acción — Identity, Origin & the Tú/Usted Choice
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You know the shapes of ser. Today you put them to work — and learn the one choice English never makes you make.
Ser does three big jobs: it tells who someone is (identity), where they're from (origin), and what they're like (lasting traits). Today you'll use it for all three — and meet the crucial choice between tú (informal "you") and usted (respectful "you"). In Latin America that choice is a quiet act of courtesy, and getting it right is one of the most useful habits you'll build all year.

I will be able to use ser to say who someone is, where they're from, and what they're like.

You already own the forms from yesterday: soy, eres, es, somos, son. Say them once, then think about who you talk to in a normal day — friends, family, teachers, strangers. Spanish asks you to address them differently, and today you'll learn how.

📚 PART 2 · SER PARA DESCRIBIR 8 min

Listen: describing with ser

Click the 🔊 and repeat. Notice how ser links a person to a trait or an origin.

  • Yo soy de México.origin — where I'm from
  • Ella es simpática.a trait — what she's like
  • Mi amigo es alto e inteligente.two traits joined
💡 DID YOU CATCH IT? · just notice, nothing to memorize

The last sentence says alto e inteligente, not alto y inteligente. Before a word beginning with an i- or hi- sound, the word for “and” (y) becomes e so the two words don't slur together. That's all — you won't be tested on it.

(In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting. Vosotros — Spain's informal "you all" — you'll meet later, labeled as European.)

🧠 PART 4 · ¿TÚ O USTED? 12 min

Two ways to say "you"

English has one word — you. Spanish gives you two, and the choice signals your relationship to the person.

TÚ — informal "you"

For friends, classmates, family, and people your own age.

¿De dónde eres? (Where are you from?)
Tú eres mi amigo. (You're my friend.)

USTED — respectful "you"

For elders, teachers, strangers, and anyone you show respect.

¿De dónde es usted? (Where are you from?)
Usted es muy amable. (You are very kind.)

The shortcut: friend or peer → tú eres. Someone older or someone you'd show respect → usted es (usted uses the same form as él/ella). Hear the respectful version: ¿De dónde es usted?

🌎 CULTURA · TÚ Y USTED
Respect you can hear

Across Latin America, using usted with an elder, a shopkeeper, or someone you've just met is a small daily courtesy — it says “I respect you.” In some countries (like Colombia and Costa Rica) people use usted even more widely, sometimes with friends. When in doubt with someone older, start with usted; they may warmly invite you to switch to tú.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Simón Bolívar (1783–1830) led much of South America to independence, and today six nations honor him as El Libertador. Bolívar es un héroe de la independencia; es venezolano. Introducing yourself in a new language — saying out loud who you are and where you're from — takes its own small courage. That's the virtue of this whole module.
