🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · DESDE EL MÓDULO 0 10 min

Everything an introduction needs

A real introduction pulls together three things you now own. Read across the row:

Piece (from)Its jobExample
gender (Módulo 0) the article that fits a noun el nombre, la nacionalidad, el país
greetings (Módulo 0) how you open, by register ¡Hola! · Buenos días (more formal)
ser + nacionalidad (Módulo 1) who you are · where you're from Yo soy mexicano/a.

Put them in one breath: «Buenos días. Me llamo Ana. Soy cubana y soy estudiante.» — a greeting, a name, and two uses of ser.

✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check your verb choice and spelling.

1. Say: “Hi, my name is (your name). I'm a student and I'm (your nationality).”

¡Hola! Me llamo ___. Soy estudiante y soy estadounidense. Two uses of ser — identity and origin. Swap in whatever is true for you.

2. Say: “My friend is kind and intelligent.”

Mi amigo/a es amable e inteligente. "My friend" → es; and remember y becomes e before the i- sound of inteligente.

3. Say: “We are Mexican and we are hard-working.”

Somos mexicanos y somos trabajadores. "We" → somos; both words agree with the group.

🌎 CULTURA · UN CONTINENTE, MUCHAS NACIONALIDADES
Naming where you're from

Spanish has a nationality word for every country, and most follow patterns: México → mexicano, Cuba → cubano, Estados Unidos → estadounidense. Because América names the whole continent in Spanish, people across the region proudly claim their specific nationality — a reminder that identity, in any language, is something you choose to say out loud.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Retrieval practice feels harder than re-reading — and that difficulty is the point. Every time you pull a word or a form of ser out of memory instead of copying it, you make the pathway stronger. It takes courage to try before you're sure. Tú eres capaz, y hoy eres un paso más cerca del examen.
