★ Fonética — The Sounds Inside Describing Words
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written the adjectives all week. Today you say them — and you record a real description.
This is a pronunciation clinic tuned to the describing words themselves. Three sounds trip up English speakers here: the throaty j (and g before e/i) in joven, generoso; the hard g in delgado, largo; and the light, tapped r in moreno, serio. We'll rehearse each with words you already know, then you record your first graded Tarea oral — a description of a real person.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Spelling tells you the sound

Spanish is wonderfully regular: once you know a few rules, you can read a new describing word aloud and be right. Today we lock in the three that matter most for this module — and then you use them on the record.

📚 PART 2 · LA J Y LA G (E,I) 10 min

The throaty sound

The Spanish j — and g when it comes before e or i — is a soft, breathy sound made at the back of the throat, close to the h in English “hat” but with a little more friction. It is not the English “j” of “jam.” Click 🔊, then echo each word, letting the air brush the back of your mouth.

  • jovenyoung — j = throaty h (“HO-ven”)
  • generosoge = same throaty sound (“he-ne-RO-so”)
  • inteligentege before e → throaty (“-li-HEN-te”)

Rule of thumb: g is throaty only before e and i. Before a, o, u it's a hard g — which is Part 3.

🧠 PART 3 · LA G FUERTE 8 min

The hard g

Before a, o, u — or before another consonant — g is the hard g of English “go.” (And gu before e/i, as in guapo… well, guapo is gu+a, simply hard g + “wa.”) Click 🔊 and echo:

  • delgadohard g before a (“del-GA-do”)
  • largohard g before o (“LAR-go”)
  • guapohard g + “wa” (“GWA-po”)

Test yourself: is the g in gracioso hard or throaty? Say it, then reveal.

Hard. The g is followed by r (a consonant), so it's the hard g of “go”: gra-CIO-so. Throaty only happens before e or i.
🔈 PART 4 · LA R SUAVE 7 min

The single, tapped r

A single r in the middle of a word isn't rolled — it's a quick tap of the tongue against the ridge behind your top teeth, very close to the soft “d” sound English speakers make in “water” or “ladder.” Try these describing words:

  • morenoone quick tap: “mo-re-no”
  • seriolight tap: “SE-rio”
  • rubioan r at the start is a bit stronger — a small trill
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say this chain slowly, three times, keeping each sound clean: joven · generoso · delgado · largo · moreno · serio. Record yourself on your phone and play it back — the fastest way to hear whether your j is throaty and your r is a light tap.
🌎 CULTURA · ACENTOS DEL MUNDO HISPANO
One language, many voices

These sounds shift a little from place to place. The throaty j is soft and gentle in the Caribbean, stronger and raspier in Spain. A single r is a crisp tap almost everywhere. None of these is “correct” and the others “wrong” — they're regional voices of one shared language. Aim to be clear and understandable, and your accent will be welcome anywhere.

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Recording yourself takes composure — the calm, self-directed control that is self-government. Everyone's first takes feel awkward; the disciplined move is to rehearse, breathe, and press record anyway, then listen back and improve. That steady mastery of yourself is the very habit that made José de San Martín a great leader. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself describing a real person — a family member, a friend, or someone you admire. Give at least four adjectives: some about how they look (physical) and some about what they're like (personality). Use ser, and make every adjective agree with the person. Aim for 25–45 seconds.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be about a real person)

«Mi hermana se llama Lucía. Ella es alta y delgada, con el pelo largo y moreno. Es muy graciosa, generosa y un poco tímida. ¡Es una hermana trabajadora!»
(Physical: alta, delgada, pelo largo/moreno. Personality: graciosa, generosa, tímida, trabajadora — every ending agreeing with a female.)

Need to hear the target one more time before you record? Replay this model:

  • Ella es…«Ella es una chica trabajadora y graciosa.»

💡 CONSEJO · plan before you record

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.

  • Did I use at least four adjectives — some physical, some personality?
  • Does every ending agree with the person (a female → -a, a group → plural)?
