📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar — a big, systematic pull of everything you can use to describe a person.
This is your fourth meeting with the adjective system, and the first day of Week 2. It's a denser review than usual, on purpose: today you retrieve. An ear-quiz on the describing words; a spiral that reaches all the way back to Módulo 1 descriptors (alto, inteligente, simpático, trabajador) and pairs describing (ser) against feelings (estar, from Módulo 3); and family from Módulo 4. Bringing it all together is exactly what the Day-10 quiz asks of you.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize spoken describing words — new and recycled — and match them to meaning.
2
I will be able to choose between ser (who someone is) and estar (how they feel now) when describing.

✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 7 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check agreement and verb choice. It's a self-test, so honesty is where the value is.

1. Say: “My mother is tall and very generous.”

Mi madre es alta y muy generosa. ser for lasting traits; both adjectives take -a to agree with madre.

2. Say: “My cousins are funny and hard-working.” (a mixed group)

Mis primos son graciosos y trabajadores. Mixed/male group → -os on graciosos; trabajador adds -es for the plural.

3. Say: “My friend is a brave girl, but today she is nervous.”

Mi amiga es una chica valiente, pero hoy está nerviosa. valiente (Family 2, no change) with ser; the right-now feeling nerviosa switches to estar.
🌎 CULTURA · UN CUMPLIDO
Describing kindly

In Spanish-speaking cultures, describing someone's personality warmly is a way of honoring them. Calling a friend generoso/a or trabajador/a is high praise; families take real pride in being una familia unida (a close family), just like Elena's. When you describe people this module, describe them the way you'd want to be described — that's the good and the beautiful working together.

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Retrieval practice feels harder than re-reading — and that difficulty is the point. Every time you pull an ending out of memory instead of copying it, you strengthen the pathway. Choosing the harder, more useful practice — and sticking with it — is exactly the self-government José de San Martín showed when he set his own ambition aside for a greater goal. Tú eres disciplinado/a, y hoy estás un paso más cerca del examen.
