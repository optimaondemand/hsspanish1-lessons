★ A leer — Elena y el Perú
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just reading. You'll meet Elena and hear the whole adjective system at work in a real little story.
This is your third encounter with the adjective system — and the most relaxed. Instead of choosing endings in a drill, you'll notice them working inside meaningful input: a short paragraph in which Elena, a teenager from Perú, describes her family. Read it, listen to it, and let the agreement settle. Then we'll take a cultural detour to Perú — and meet the liberator behind this module's virtue.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and understand a short paragraph that describes several people with agreeing adjectives.
2
I will be able to locate Perú and name one thing it is known for.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 8 min

Before you read

Good readers guess before they read. Elena is going to describe her family — a father, a mother, sisters, a younger brother. As you read, keep one job in mind: for every adjective, notice who it agrees with and how its ending shows that (a single sister → -a; two sisters → -as).

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
If you described your own family in one word each, what would come to mind first?
📚 PART 2 · LA LECTURA · DIEGO 16 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud by a native speaker. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know most of them.

Escuchar la lectura completa — Elena

«Hola, me llamo Elena y soy del Perú. Mi familia es grande. Mi padre es alto y trabajador. Mi madre es baja, pero muy fuerte; tiene el pelo largo. Mis dos hermanas son graciosas e inteligentes. Mi hermano menor es tímido, pero generoso y valiente. Casi todos somos morenos y tenemos los ojos grandes. ¡Somos una familia muy unida!»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

me llamo = my name is · del Perú = from Peru · grande = big · pero = but · tiene = has · el pelo largo = long hair · dos = two · e inteligentes = and intelligent (y becomes e before an “ee” sound) · menor = younger · casi todos = almost all of us · los ojos grandes = big eyes · unida = close / united
✅ PART 3 · ¿COMPRENDISTE? 16 min

Check your understanding

Answer from the paragraph. These are reading questions — you're showing you understood, not choosing an ending.

✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Cómo es el padre de Elena? (What is Elena's father like?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Cómo son las dos hermanas? (What are the two sisters like?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Quién es tímido? (Who is shy?)

Look closely: why is it hermanas graciosas but hermano tímido? Say it out loud, then reveal.

graciosas agrees with dos hermanas — feminine and plural, so -as. tímido agrees with one brother — masculine singular, so -o. Same rule, different people: the ending always answers to the noun.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Be a detective. Go back through the paragraph and find every adjective. For each one, whisper who it describes and why the ending is what it is. You'll find all three families in there — -o/-a (alto, graciosas), -e/consonant (grande, fuerte, valiente), and the -or word (trabajador). This is the same skill the Day-10 quiz will ask for — here it's just noticing.
🌎 CULTURA · EL PERÚ
El país de Elena

Perú sits on the Pacific coast of South America, its capital Lima spread along the shore while the Andes rise behind it and the Amazon rainforest stretches east. High in those mountains stands Machu Picchu, the stone city of the Inca — one of the most famous places on Earth.

Alongside Spanish, millions of Peruvians speak quechua, the language of the Inca, still alive today. And Peruvian food is world-famous: ceviche — fresh fish “cooked” in lime juice — is the national dish. La comida peruana es deliciosa.

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT
José de San Martín — el Libertador del Sur

José de San Martín led armies across the towering Andes to free Argentina, Chile, and Perú from colonial rule — and in 1821, in Lima, he declared Perú's independence. His greatest act of self-government came next: rather than fight Simón Bolívar for control of the liberated lands, he stepped away from power and sailed to Europe, putting the cause above his own ambition. Mastering yourself — your pride, your hunger for glory — can take more courage than winning a battle. Él era un líder valiente, honesto y muy disciplinado.

ⓘ Don't confuse him with San Martín de Porres — the humble Peruvian friar you met in Módulo 5. Same country, two very different “San Martíns.”
