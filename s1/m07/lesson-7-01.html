★ ¿Cómo es? — Describing How People Look
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Welcome to Módulo 7. The whole module answers one question: How do I describe people?
You've been describing people in small ways since Módulo 1 — alto, inteligente, simpático. This module makes it systematic. We start with the easy half: physical appearance — tall, thin, dark hair, blue eyes. There's no new grammar today on purpose; we're stocking up on describing words so that when the full adjective system arrives this week, it has plenty to stand on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name common words for physical appearance in Spanish.
2
I will be able to talk about someone's hair (el pelo) and eyes (los ojos).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Picture a person

Before any Spanish, think of someone you'd describe to a friend — a family member, a favorite character, an athlete. What would you say about how they look? By the end of this module you'll be able to say all of it in Spanish. Let's begin with the words for looks.

📚 PART 2 · EL ASPECTO FÍSICO 15 min

Listen, then repeat

Click the 🔊 and say each word back out loud. Many of these end in -o/-a and change to match the person: a boy is guapo, a girl is guapa. You met this agreement idea in Módulo 0 — this week we make it a full system. A few words, like fuerte, stay the same for everyone.

  • guapo/agood-looking / handsome
  • bonito/apretty
  • feo/aplain / ugly
  • delgado/athin / slim
  • fuertestrong
  • moreno/adark-haired / dark-skinned
  • rubio/ablond / fair-haired

Hair and eyes — and words for length

  • el pelohair
  • los ojoseyes
  • corto/ashort (in length)
  • largo/along

Heads up: largo is a “false friend” — it means long, not “large.” And corto means short in length (short hair = pelo corto); for a short person you'll use bajo, from Módulo 1.

🌎 CULTURA · DESCRIBIR CON RESPETO
Moreno — a warm, everyday word

Across Latin America, moreno/a is a common, friendly way to describe someone with dark hair or a brown complexion — it carries none of the awkwardness the English “dark” sometimes does. Spanish speakers describe appearance easily and kindly; a warm «¡Qué guapo!» is a compliment you'll hear at any family gathering.
