★ A leer — Carla y su día en San Juan
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just reading. You'll meet Carla and hear the -ar verbs working inside a real school day.
This is your third encounter with the -ar pattern — and the most relaxed. Instead of choosing endings in a drill, you'll simply notice the verbs at work inside meaningful input: a short paragraph about a student in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Read it, listen to it, and let the pattern settle. Then we'll take a quick cultural detour to her island.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and understand a short paragraph built from -ar verbs and school words.
2
I will be able to locate Puerto Rico and name one thing it is known for.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 8 min

Before you read

Good readers guess before they read. Carla is a teenager in San Juan, the capital of Puerto Rico. As you go into the paragraph, keep one question in mind: which words tell you what she does (an action → an -ar verb), and which tells you who she is (identity → ser)?

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think about your own school morning. Which is most true for you?
📚 PART 2 · LA LECTURA · CARLA 16 min

Listen first, then read

Click 🔊 to hear the whole paragraph read aloud by a native speaker. Listen once with your eyes closed, then read along a second time. Don't worry about every word — you already know the important ones.

Escuchar la lectura completa — Carla

«Hola, me llamo Carla. Soy de San Juan, Puerto Rico. Todos los días llego a la escuela a las ocho. Estudio mucho y hablo español e inglés. En la clase de ciencias, la maestra enseña y nosotros escuchamos. Practico el piano y uso mi computadora para la tarea. Mi mamá trabaja en un hospital. ¡Me gusta mi escuela!»

Not sure of a word? Reveal a mini-glossary — but try to read without it first.

me llamo = my name is · todos los días = every day · llego = I arrive · a las ocho = at eight o'clock · mucho = a lot · e inglés = and English · enseña = teaches · escuchamos = we listen · practico = I practice · uso = I use · trabaja = works · me gusta = I like
✅ PART 3 · ¿COMPRENDISTE? 16 min

Check your understanding

Answer from the paragraph. These are reading questions — you're showing you understood.

✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿De dónde es Carla? (Where is Carla from?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Qué enseña la maestra? (What does the teacher teach?)
✅ TRY IT · from the reading
¿Dónde trabaja la mamá de Carla? (Where does Carla's mom work?)

In your own words: why does Carla say soy de San Juan but estudio mucho? Say it out loud, then reveal.

Soy de San Juan is who she is / where she's from — identity, so ser. Estudio mucho is something she does — an action, so just the -ar verb. Two different jobs, two different verbs.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go back to the paragraph and be a detective. Find every -ar verb (there are seven: llego, estudio, hablo, enseña, escuchamos, practico, uso, trabaja) and the one ser (soy). For each -ar verb, whisper who's doing it — yo, ella, or nosotros? This is the same skill the Day 10 quiz will ask for — but here it's just noticing.
🌎 CULTURA · PUERTO RICO
San Juan y la Isla del Encanto

Carla is from the Caribbean island of Puerto Rico, nicknamed «la Isla del Encanto» (the Island of Enchantment). Its capital, San Juan, is one of the oldest cities under the U.S. flag — its stone fort, El Morro, has guarded the harbor for over 400 years. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory, so its people are U.S. citizens, yet Spanish is the language of home, school, and the street.

The island gave the world reggaetón and the older, elegant bomba and plena rhythms — music where Spanish, African, and Taíno roots meet. Students like Carla estudian, hablan, and practican in two languages, moving between them all day long.

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Notice that Carla's mother trabaja en un hospital — she spends her day helping others heal. Puerto Rico's greatest hero, Roberto Clemente, lived the same value on a global stage: a baseball legend who used his fame to carry food and relief to people in crisis. Whether in a San Juan hospital or on a relief flight, service is putting your work at the service of someone else's need. Nosotros ayudamos a los demás.
