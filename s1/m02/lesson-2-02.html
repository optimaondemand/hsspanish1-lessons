Lesson 2.02
★ ¿Qué haces? — School Verbs & the -ar Present
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the things. Today you learn the actions — and the single pattern behind almost all of them.
First you'll pick up a set of everyday school verbs (study, speak, listen, work…). Almost every one ends in -ar — and Spanish treats them all the same way. Meet that pattern today through one model verb, estudiar (to study). We're learning the -ar present tense on its own today — its five endings, riding on the school words you already know. One new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name common school actions using -ar verbs.
2
I will be able to use the five present-tense forms of estudiar (estudio, estudias, estudia, estudiamos, estudian).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

What do you actually do at school?

Think in plain English for a second: on a normal school day you read, you listen, you practice, maybe you work a job after. Every one of those is a verb — and by the end of this lesson you'll have Spanish for them. Start by noticing your own day.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which do you spend the most time doing on a school day?
📚 PART 2 · LOS VERBOS -AR 12 min

Listen, then repeat

Here are ten school verbs in their infinitive form — the “to ___” form, before you change it for a subject. Every one ends in -ar. Click the 🔊 and say each back. Notice the shared -ar ending: that's your clue they all follow one pattern.

  • estudiarto study
  • hablarto speak / talk
  • escucharto listen
  • trabajarto work
  • practicarto practice
  • llegarto arrive
  • enseñarto teach
  • tomarto take / drink
  • necesitarto need
  • usarto use
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go through the list again and pair each verb with a noun from yesterday: estudiar + el libro, usar + la mochila, tomar + el examen. Saying the two together (“study the book,” “take the exam”) makes both stick faster.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PRESENTE DE ESTUDIAR 10 min

Meet the -ar present tense

A verb changes its ending to match who is doing it. Take off the -ar from estudiar to get the stem estudi-, then add the ending for each subject. Click the 🔊 in each row to hear the example, then repeat it aloud.

SubjectendingestudiarListen & repeat
yo-oestudio Yo estudio español. (I study Spanish.)
tú-asestudias ¿Tú estudias mucho? (Do you study a lot?)
usted / él / ella-aestudia Ella estudia en casa. (She studies at home.)
nosotros/as-amosestudiamos Nosotros estudiamos juntos. (We study together.)
ustedes / ellos / ellas-anestudian Ellos estudian ciencias. (They study science.)

(In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting — formal or casual. Vosotros, Spain's informal "you all," you'll meet later, labeled as European. The big idea: learn these five endings once and you can conjugate hablar, trabajar, escuchar — every regular -ar verb — the exact same way.)

✅ PART 4 · A PRACTICAR 11 min

Pick the right form of estudiar

You're only choosing which ending matches the subject. Glance back at the table above if you need it.

✅ TRY IT · which form?
"Yo ___ español." (I ___ Spanish.)
✅ TRY IT · which form?
"Nosotros ___ juntos." (We ___ together.)

Your turn: how would you say "They study science"? Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Ellos estudian ciencias. "They" → the -an ending: estudian. Same ending you'll use for ustedes and ellas.
🌎 CULTURA · PUERTO RICO
La escuela en la isla

In Puerto Rico, students estudian in both Spanish and English — the island is bilingual, and most schools teach both languages every day. A student in San Juan might say «Estudio matemáticas en español y hablo inglés con mis amigos.» Two languages, one school day.
