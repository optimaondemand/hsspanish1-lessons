★ Fonética — Sounding Like Yourself in Spanish
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written the -ar verbs all week. Today you say them — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic. Spanish sounds are more regular than English, and three habits carry most of a good accent: pure, short vowels; predictable stress; and the sound of the ñ (you meet it in enseñar and español). We'll rehearse each one out loud using words you already know, then you'll record your first graded Tarea oral — three true sentences describing what you do at school.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pronounce the five pure Spanish vowels, place word stress correctly, and say the ñ.
2
I will be able to record three true sentences describing my daily school activities with -ar verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why pronunciation is the easy part

Here's good news: Spanish spelling almost always tells you exactly how to say a word. There are no silent letters lurking the way English has them in knight or through. If you learn a few sound rules, you can read a brand-new word aloud and be right. Today we lock in those rules — and then you use them on air.

📚 PART 2 · LAS CINCO VOCALES 11 min

Five vowels, one clean sound each

English vowels slide and stretch (say "no" slowly and you'll hear it drift to "noh-oo"). Spanish vowels do not glide — each is short, crisp, and always the same. Keep them clipped.

VowelSay it like…Keep it…
athe a in "father"short, open
ethe e in "bet"short, no glide to "ay"
ithe ee in "see" (but quick)tight, brief
othe o in "for"round, no glide to "ow"
uthe oo in "food" (but quick)short, lips forward

Model words — listen, then echo

These are all words you already know from this module. Click 🔊, then say each one back, clipping every vowel. Listen for how steady each vowel stays.

  • la claseclean a and e, no glide
  • el librotight i, round o
  • la mochilaopen o, tight i, open a
  • estudiarclean e, forward u, tight i
  • usarforward u + open a
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say the vowel chain out loud, slowly, five times: a – e – i – o – u. Then replay la mochila and see if you can match all three of its vowels exactly. Recording yourself on your phone and playing it back is the single fastest way to hear your own glides.
🧠 PART 3 · EL ACENTO (STRESS) 9 min

Which syllable do I punch?

Spanish stress follows two simple defaults — and a written accent mark (tilde) overrides them:

  • Word ends in a vowel, -n, or -s → stress the second-to-last syllable. es-CUE-la, mo-CHI-la, es-TU-dio, e-XA-men.
  • Word ends in any other consonant → stress the last syllable. tra-ba-JAR, es-tu-DIAR, u-SAR.
  • A written accent beats both rules — just stress the marked vowel. examen needs no mark (it ends in -n, so the default already stresses e-XA-men), but its plural does: ex-Á-me-nes — without the mark, the default would stress -ME-.

This is why the infinitives punch the end — tra-ba-JAR — but the conjugated forms fall back to the default. Listen:

  • Yo hablo inglés y español.HA-blo (default), es-pa-ÑOL (ends in -l)
  • Mi maestra trabaja en la escuela.tra-BA-ja, es-CUE-la — both default
  • Nosotros estudiamos juntos.es-tu-DIA-mos — ends in -s, default

The word mochila ends in a vowel. Say it out loud and decide which syllable you punch — then reveal.

Stress the second-to-last syllable: mo-CHI-la. It ends in a vowel, so the default rule applies — no accent mark needed.

Say trabajar out loud — where does the stress go, and why? Decide, then reveal.

tra-ba-JAR — it ends in -r (a consonant that isn't n or s), so the default lands the stress on the last syllable. No accent mark needed.
🔈 PART 4 · LA Ñ 5 min

The one letter English doesn't have

The ñ is its own letter in the Spanish alphabet, and it makes the ny sound you already say in English "canyon" or "onion." You need it for a key school verb — try these out loud:

  • enseñar (to teach) → en-se-ÑAR
  • español (Spanish) → es-pa-ÑOL
  • la mañana (morning) → ma-ÑA-na

You've already used the ñ every time you said español and in the friendly sign-off ¡hasta mañana! Now you know it's a whole letter with a sound of its own.

🌎 CULTURA · LA Ñ
Una letra con historia

Medieval scribes writing Latin doubled the n in words like annus (year); to save costly parchment they began writing a small n above the first — that little wave became the tilde, and ñ was born. Today the letter is a proud symbol of the Spanish language itself: it appears in España, in the logo of the Instituto Cervantes, and in the very word español.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Speaking a new language out loud — and recording it — takes courage. Everyone's first recordings feel awkward; that feeling is not a sign you're doing it wrong, it's a sign you're doing it at all. Rehearse, breathe, and press record. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your first graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself saying three true sentences about what you do at school on a normal day. Use at least three different -ar verbs (for example estudiar, hablar, escuchar, practicar, usar, llegar). Together they should describe your daily school activities. Aim for 20–40 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. Soy estudiante en Optima. Todos los días llego a la clase y estudio español. Practico el piano y uso mi computadora para la tarea.»
(That's four different -ar verbs — llegar, estudiar, practicar, usar — all describing a real school day.)

Need help hearing the target verbs one more time before you record? Replay these models:

  • Yo estudio español.estudiar — yo form
  • Yo hablo inglés y español.hablar — yo form
  • Mi maestra trabaja en la escuela.trabajar — ella form

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.
