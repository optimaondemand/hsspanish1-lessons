★ Repaso y espiral — Retrieval Before the Quiz
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new grammar today — just pulling it back out of your head, which is where real learning happens.
This is your fourth meeting with the -ar verbs, and the first day of Week 2. Instead of new material, today you retrieve: an ear-quiz on the school words, a short production prompt, and a spiral that puts two verbs side by side — ser (from Módulo 1, who someone is) and the regular -ar verbs (this module, what someone does). Telling those two apart is exactly what the Day-10 quiz will ask of you.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize spoken school words and match them to meaning.
2
I will be able to choose between ser and an -ar verb depending on the job the sentence is doing.

🧠 PART 3 · ESPIRAL · SER vs. -AR 10 min

Two verbs, two jobs

You now know two ways to talk about people. They are not interchangeable — each one does a different job. Listen and read across the row:

Verb (from)Its jobExample · listen
ser (Módulo 1) who someone is · role · origin Yo soy estudiante.
-ar verb (Módulo 2) an action someone does Yo estudio español.

Hear both in one breath: Yo soy estudiante y estudio español. One ser (who I am) and one -ar verb (what I do) — side by side, never glued together. That's the whole spiral.

✅ SPIRAL CHECK · ser or an -ar verb?
"Nosotros ___ de Puerto Rico." (We ___ from Puerto Rico.) — it's origin.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · ser or an -ar verb?
"Ella ___ inglés en la clase." (She ___ English in class.) — it's an action.
✅ SPIRAL CHECK · ser or an -ar verb?
"Mi maestra ___ muy amable." (My teacher ___ very kind.) — it's identity / a trait.
✍️ PART 4 · A PRODUCIR 8 min

Build it yourself, then check

For each prompt, say the full Spanish sentence out loud first — commit before you peek. Then reveal to check your verb choice and spelling. This is a self-test, so being honest with yourself is where the value is.

1. Say: “I am a student and I study Spanish every day.”

Soy estudiante y estudio español todos los días. Identity → soy (ser); an action → estudio (-ar verb). Two jobs, two verbs — not “soy estudio.”

2. Say: “My mom works and speaks two languages.”

Mi mamá trabaja y habla dos idiomas. Two actions → two -ar verbs; “she” → the -a ending on both (trabaja, habla).

3. Say: “We arrive at school and we listen to the teacher.”

Nosotros llegamos a la escuela y escuchamos a la maestra. Two actions → the -amos ending for “we” on both (llegamos, escuchamos).
📖 PART 5 · A LEER OTRA VEZ 5 min

Carla returns — listen and read

You met this passage on Day 5. Press play, follow along, and this time notice how ser and the -ar verbs take turns doing their jobs.

Play the reading

«Hola, me llamo Carla. Soy de San Juan, Puerto Rico. Todos los días llego a la escuela a las ocho. Estudio mucho y hablo español e inglés. En la clase de ciencias, la maestra enseña y nosotros escuchamos. Practico el piano y uso mi computadora para la tarea. Mi mamá trabaja en un hospital. ¡Me gusta mi escuela!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Point to each verb and say its job out loud: soy (who she is), estudio / hablo / practico / uso (what she does), enseña (what the teacher does), escuchamos (what they do together). Only one is ser — the rest are all -ar actions.
🌎 CULTURA · EL ESPAÑOL Y EL INGLÉS
Una isla bilingüe

In Puerto Rico, students like Carla move between Spanish and English all day — estudian in one language, hablan the other with friends, and often mix both. It's a real-world reminder that ser (who you are, puertorriqueño/a) stays constant, while what you do — the -ar verbs — fills the day in two languages at once.

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Retrieval practice feels harder than re-reading — and that difficulty is the point. Every time you pull ser or an -ar verb out of your memory instead of copying it, you make the pathway stronger. Think of Roberto Clemente, who trained relentlessly and then gave his skill away in service to others. Doing the harder, more useful practice today is a small act of service to your future self. Tú trabajas hoy para el examen de mañana.
