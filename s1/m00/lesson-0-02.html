Lesson 0.02
★ El alfabeto — Spell Your Name in Spanish
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday: five vowels. Today: all 27 letters — including one that doesn't exist in English.
The Spanish alphabet (el abecedario) has the same 26 letters as English plus the ñ. Most letters sound close to what you'd expect; a handful have their own names you'll want to hear. By the end you'll be able to do something genuinely useful from day one: spell your own name out loud in Spanish — exactly what you'd do to sign up for anything in a Spanish-speaking country.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the letters of the Spanish alphabet, including the special ñ (eñe).
2
I will be able to spell my own name aloud, letter by letter, in Spanish.

Before the full alphabet, run yesterday's five vowels once more — they're the sounds hiding inside every letter name. Say them out loud: a, e, i, o, u.

📚 PART 2 · EL ABECEDARIO 15 min

All 27 letters — listen and echo

Click each 🔊 to hear the letter's name and say it back. Don't memorize all 27 at once — just get the shape of them, and spend extra time on the ones that surprise you.

  • G g“ge”

  • I i“i”

  • Z z“zeta”
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Three letters trip up English speakers the most: h (hache) is always silent, j (jota) sounds like a breathy English h, and ñ (eñe) is its own letter with the "ny" sound. Replay just those three 🔊 and say each letter name five times.
✅ PART 3 · DELETREA TU NOMBRE 12 min

Spell your own name aloud

In Spanish, when someone asks «¿Cómo se escribe?» (How is it spelled?), you answer letter by letter using the names you just learned. Write your first name on paper, then say each letter's Spanish name out loud. For example, ANA is a — ene — a, and LUIS is ele — u — i — ese. Replay any letter's 🔊 above if you get stuck.

🌎 CULTURA · LA Ñ
La letra que es puro español

The ñ was born from thrifty medieval scribes: to save parchment, they wrote a small n above another n, and that little wave (la tilde) became a letter of its own. Today the ñ is a proud emblem of the Spanish language — it's even in the word español itself. Around the world, more than 500 million people use this alphabet every day.
