Lesson 0.05
★ El género y los artículos — The Word for “The”
Lesson · about 40 minutes · closes Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One idea today unlocks the whole course: in Spanish, every noun has a gender, and the word for “the” changes to match.
This is the foundation everything is built on. Spanish nouns are either masculine or feminine — not because objects have a gender, but simply as a grammar category. The word for "the" comes in four flavors: el, la, los, las. The golden rule: learn every noun glued to its article. You'll also collect free vocabulary (cognates) and take a first look at the whole Spanish-speaking world.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

A new idea for English speakers

In English, "the" never changes — the dog, the house, the cats. Spanish is different, and that surprises everyone at first. There's nothing to be afraid of; it's just a new habit.

📚 PART 2 · EL / LA / LOS / LAS 15 min

Four words for “the”

Which one you use depends on two things: is the noun masculine or feminine, and is it singular (one) or plural (more than one)?

MasculineFeminine
Singular (one)el gato (the cat)la casa (the house)
Plural (more)los gatos (the cats)las casas (the houses)

A very handy hint: nouns ending in -o are usually masculine (el libro), and nouns ending in -a are usually feminine (la mesa). It's not a perfect rule — el día (the day) and la mano (the hand) break it — which is exactly why you learn each noun with its article.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Look around the room. For five objects you can name in English, guess the Spanish gender from the “-o = el, -a = la” hint. You won't know all the words yet — that's fine. The point is to start thinking in the two-color system: is it an el or a la?
🎁 PART 3 · COGNADOS Y FRASES ÚTILES 13 min

Free words you already know

A cognate (cognado) is a word that looks and means nearly the same in both languages — free vocabulary! Notice each still takes a gendered article:

  • la familia · el animal · la música · el hospital · la universidad

Careful with “false friends”: ropa means clothes (not rope), and librería is a bookstore (not library). Most cognates are your friends, though — use them.

FRASES ÚTILES · two chunks for talking about yourself

  • soy de…I'm from… (soy de los Estados Unidos)
  • tengo… añosI'm… years old (lit. "I have… years")

Notice: in Spanish you don't are a certain age — you have years. Tengo quince años = "I have fifteen years" = I'm 15. (You'll learn all your numbers next week.)

🌎 CULTURA DESTACADA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Welcome to a world of Spanish

Spanish isn't the language of one country — it's the everyday language of more than 20 nations and over 500 million people, making it one of the most spoken languages on Earth. It's official across most of Latin America — from Mexico (the country with the most Spanish speakers of all) south through Central America, the Caribbean (Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico), and nearly all of South America (Colombia, Peru, Argentina, Chile, and more) — and it's also the language of Spain, where it began.

That reach is why this course takes a Latin-American-first, pan-Hispanic view: you'll hear a clear, neutral accent understood everywhere, and meet food, music, and traditions from across the whole Spanish-speaking world. On Day 9 you'll explore all of it on an interactive map. For now, just take in how far your new language can carry you.
