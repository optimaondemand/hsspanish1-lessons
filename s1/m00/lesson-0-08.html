Lesson 0.08
★ Fonética — Speak Spanish on the Record
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've heard and repeated all week. Today you record yourself — your first graded speaking.
First a quick fonética recap: the five pure vowels, the silent h, and the ñ. Then you'll record your first Tarea oral — four simple things you can already do: spell your name, give a greeting, count 0–10, and say one chunk about yourself. Everyone's first recording feels a little strange — that's normal, and you'll rehearse out loud before the mic ever turns on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pronounce the five pure vowels, keep the h silent, and say the ñ.
2
I will be able to record myself spelling my name, greeting, counting 0–10, and saying one chunk about me.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Recording yourself feels weird — that's okay

Nobody loves the sound of their own voice at first, and speaking a new language into a mic feels awkward for everyone. That feeling isn't a sign you're doing it wrong — it's a sign you're actually doing it. Name how you feel, then we rehearse.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
How do you feel about recording yourself in Spanish?
📚 PART 2 · REPASO DE FONÉTICA 12 min

Three habits for a clean accent

HabitRuleExample
Pure vowelsEach vowel = one short, steady sound, no glide.a-e-i-o-u
Silent hThe letter h is never pronounced.hola = "OH-la"
The ñSay the "ny" in "canyon."niño = "NEE-nyo"

Warm up your voice on the sounds you'll need — play each 🔊 and repeat:

  • a – e – i – o – uthe five pure vowels (play each letter)
  • holasilent h — "OH-la"
  • ñ (eñe)the "ny" letter
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Record a 10-second warm-up on your phone: say the five vowels, then hola, then niño. Play it back and listen for any English glide on the vowels. Fixing it here means a stronger Tarea oral in a moment.
🎤 PART 3 · ENSAYA LAS CUATRO PARTES 8 min

Rehearse each piece before you record

Your recording has four short parts. Practice each one aloud now, using the models:

  • 1. Spell your nameletter by letter (h is silent; ñ is eñe)
  • 2. Give a greetinge.g. «¡Hola! Buenos días.»
  • 3. Count 0–10cero, uno, dos… diez
  • 4. Say a chunk about youe.g. «Me llamo ___» or «Soy de ___»
✅ ONE LAST CHECK · before you record
When you spell a name with an h in it, on the recording you should…

(The h is silent inside words like hola — but when you spell aloud, you still name the letter: hache.)

👋 A WORD OF ENCOURAGEMENT

One week ago you had never made a Spanish sound. Today you're about to record yourself spelling, greeting, counting, and introducing yourself — in a language spoken by half a billion people. Be proud of that, press record, and don't chase perfection. Every speaker you admire once sounded exactly like a beginner. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 4 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your first graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself doing all four things, in order: (1) spell your first name letter by letter, (2) give a greeting, (3) count from 0 to 10, and (4) say one true chunk about yourself (me llamo… or soy de…). Aim for about 30–45 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Me llamo Ana — a, ene, a. ¡Hola, buenos días! Cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez. Soy de México.»
(Name spelled · a greeting · 0–10 · one chunk about me.)

💡 CONSEJO · plan before you record

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.

How it's scored — the rubric

Three criteria, 5 points each, 15 total. Read it before you record so you know what earns full marks.

CriterionWhat earns 5 / 5Pts
Task CompletionAll four parts present — name spelled, a greeting, 0–10 counted, and one true chunk about you.5
Language AccuracyCorrect letter names, numbers in order, and an accurate greeting and chunk.5
Pronunciation & FluencyClean, short vowels; silent h; the ñ where it belongs; steady pace, understandable throughout.5
✅ SELF-REVIEW · check these before you submit
  • Did I include all four parts, in order?
  • Did I spell my name with the Spanish letter names (not English)?
  • Did I count 0 to 10 without skipping?
  • Is my chunk true about me (me llamo… or soy de…)?
  • Did I keep my vowels short and the h silent in hola?
  • Did I play my recording back once and re-record if needed before submitting?
