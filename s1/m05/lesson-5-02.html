★ Me, te, le… — The Little Words Behind Gustar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before we can say what we like, we need one small piece of the machine: the pronouns me, te, le, nos, les.
First you'll pick up the verbs of preference — gustar (to like), encantar (to love), cocinar (to cook), and the adjective favorito. Then, learning them on their own today, you'll meet five little words that tell us who likes something: me (to me), te (to you), le (to him/her/you-formal), nos (to us), les (to them / you all). The full gustar pattern — how these snap together with the verb — waits until tomorrow. One new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use the verbs gustar, encantar, cocinar and the word favorito.
2
I will be able to match each pronoun — me, te, le, nos, les — to the person it points to.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You already say this in English

English has a version of these pronouns too — to me, to you, to us. When you say “give the ball to me,” that me is exactly what Spanish packs into a single little word. Today you're just learning the five Spanish versions and who each one points at.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Whose food preferences do you know best?
📚 PART 2 · LOS VERBOS DEL GUSTO 12 min

Listen, then repeat

Four words that let you talk about preferences. Click the 🔊 and say each one back out loud.

  • gustarto like (lit. “to be pleasing”)
  • encantarto love / really like
  • cocinarto cook
  • favorito/afavorite

Notice gustar is glossed “to be pleasing” — keep that in your back pocket. It's the key to why the whole pattern looks “backwards,” which you'll unlock tomorrow. And favorito/a is an adjective, so it agrees: mi comida favorita, mi jugo favorito.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say your comida favorita and your bebida favorita out loud, using yesterday's food words: Mi comida favorita es el pollo. Mi bebida favorita es el jugo. (You already know es from ser!)
🧠 PART 3 · LOS PRONOMBRES 10 min

Five words for “to whom”

These pronouns answer one question: the liking belongs to whom? Learn them as a set — they're the same five you'll use with gustar and encantar tomorrow. Press 🔊 to hear all five read in order.

Listen: me · te · le · nos · les

PronounPoints to…Think of it as…
meyo (I)to me
tetú (you, informal)to you
leusted / él / ellato him / her / you (formal)
nosnosotros/as (we)to us
lesustedes / ellos / ellasto them / you all

(In Latin America, les covers “you all” for any group. Spain also uses os for an informal “you all” — a European form you'll meet later.)

le. le points to usted / él / ella — one other person. Tomorrow you'll build the whole sentence: Le gusta el café.
★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

Courtesy often lives in the smallest words. Asking «¿Te gusta?» (do you like it?) before you assume — and choosing le for someone older to show respect — is a quiet way of putting the other person first. These little pronouns are how Spanish keeps the other person in view.
