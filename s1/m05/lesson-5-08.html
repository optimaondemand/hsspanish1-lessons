📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written me gusta all week. Today you say it — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic, and the food words are perfect practice because they hide three sounds English handles differently: the throaty j (as in jugo), the rolled rr (as in arroz), and the ll (as in pollo). We'll rehearse each out loud with words you already know, then you'll record your Tarea oral — three things you like and one you don't.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Three sounds worth getting right

Spanish spelling is reliable — once you know how a letter sounds, it sounds that way every time. Today's three sounds are the ones English speakers most often need to adjust, and they live right in the food you'll be talking about.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you say Spanish food words out loud, what feels trickiest?
📚 PART 2 · LA JOTA (J) 10 min

The throaty j

The Spanish j is not the English “j” in “jam.” It's a soft, breathy sound made in the back of the throat — a bit like the “h” in “hello,” but stronger. Click 🔊, listen, and echo it.

  • el jugothe j = throaty h: HOO-go
  • los frijolesfri-HO-les — same throaty j

(The letter g before e or i makes the same throaty sound — think of the name Jorge, where both the J and the g sound alike.)

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Say a whole sentence: «Me gusta el jugo, y me gustan los frijoles.» Feel the throaty j land twice. Recording it on your phone and playing it back is the fastest way to hear whether you're using an English “j” by accident.
🧠 PART 3 · LA DOBLE ERRE (RR) 10 min

The rolled rr

The double rr is a quick trill — the tip of your tongue flutters against the ridge behind your top teeth. A helpful trick: say the English phrase “pot of tea” fast (“poTTaTea”) — that quick tongue-tap is close to a Spanish r. Now do several in a row for the trill. Listen and echo:

  • el arroza-RRR-oz — roll the double r
  • A single r between vowels is one quick tap: fru-ta, pe-ro.
  • A double rr — or an r at the start of a word — is a full trill: arroz.

Which one has the rolled trill — the double-r word or the single-r word? Decide, then reveal.

The trill is in arroz (double r). Fruta has a single r — just one quick tap, no roll. Don't worry if your trill is shaky at first; even a clear single tap is understandable.
🔈 PART 4 · LA DOBLE ELE (LL) 5 min

The ll sounds like “y”

In most of Latin America, the double ll sounds like the English y in “yes.” So pollo is PO-yo, not “PO-lo.” Try it:

  • el polloPO-yo — the ll = y

You've already met this sound: gallo pinto from Day 5 is GA-yo. Same ll.

🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
«¡Buen provecho!»

Before eating in Costa Rica — and across the Spanish-speaking world — people say «¡Buen provecho!», roughly “enjoy your meal.” It's said to family at home and even to strangers as you pass their table in a soda (a small Costa Rican eatery). Notice the sounds you just practiced hiding in the food: arroz, frijoles, pollo. Say them well and a tico will smile.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Speaking a new language out loud — and recording it — takes courage. Everyone's first recordings feel awkward; that feeling is not a sign you're doing it wrong, it's a sign you're doing it at all. Rehearse, breathe, and press record. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself saying three things you like and one thing you don't, all about food and drinks. Use me gusta or me gustan for what you like, at least one me encanta for something you love, and no me gusta for the one you don't. Aim for 20–40 seconds total.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. Me encanta el arroz con frijoles. También me gusta el café y me gustan las frutas. Pero no me gusta la leche.»
(That's one encanta, one gusta, one gustan — three things I like — and one no me gusta.)

Need to hear the target patterns one more time before you record? Replay these models:

  • Me gusta el café.like — one thing
  • Me gustan las verduras.like — several things
  • Me encanta el arroz con frijoles.love — stronger
✅ ONE LAST CHECK · before you record
To say “I don't like milk,” which is correct?
💡 CONSEJO · plan before you record

If it helps you, write out your sentences first and read them aloud a couple of times before you record. Many students sound calmer and clearer once they’ve drafted a quick script — reading from your notes is completely fine. Just aim to sound natural, not rushed.
