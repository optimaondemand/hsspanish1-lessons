★ ¿Quién está en mi familia? — Family Members
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Welcome to Módulo 4. Our essential question is a warm one: Who is in my family?
This module is about la familia — the people closest to you — and about telling ages. Today we start with the easy, human half: the names of family members. Mother, father, brother, sister, grandparents, children, cousin. There's no new grammar today on purpose: we're stocking up on people-words so that tomorrow, when the verb tener (to have) arrives, you'll already have a whole family for it to talk about.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the members of a family in Spanish.
2
I will be able to learn each family word with its article (la, el, los) and its gender.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Picture the people

Before any Spanish, picture the people you'd count as your family — the ones you live with, talk to, or grew up around. Families come in every shape, and all of them count here. Let's start by thinking about who is closest to you.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Who do you talk to the most in your family?
📚 PART 2 · LA FAMILIA 14 min

Listen, then repeat

Click the 🔊 and say each word back out loud. Notice the article in front (la, el, los) — that's the grammatical gender you met in Módulo 0. Learn each person with the article.

Parents & siblings

  • la familiathe family
  • la madremother
  • el padrefather
  • los padresparents
  • el hermanobrother
  • la hermanasister

Grandparents, children & cousins

  • el abuelograndfather
  • la abuelagrandmother
  • el hijoson
  • la hijadaughter
  • el primocousin

Notice the pattern: many family words come in an -o / -a pair — hermano / hermana, hijo / hija, abuelo / abuela, primo / prima. The masculine plural also covers a mixed group: los padres = the parents, los hermanos = the siblings, los abuelos = the grandparents.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Think of your own family and name each real person out loud in Spanish: mi madre, mi padre, mi hermano… Even if you only have a few members, say each one aloud. Attaching a word to a real face makes it stick.
