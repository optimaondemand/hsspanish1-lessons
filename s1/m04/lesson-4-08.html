★ Fonética — La r, la h muda y la ñ
Lesson + graded oral task · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've written about your family all week. Today you say them out loud — and you record it.
This is a pronunciation clinic built around the family words themselves. Three sounds carry most of a good accent here: the soft, tapped r in the middle of words like padre and primo; the silent h in hermano, hija, and hambre; and the ñ you'll need for años. We'll rehearse each one aloud using words you already know, then you'll record your first graded Tarea oral — introducing your family and giving their ages.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to tap a single Spanish r, keep the h silent, and say the ñ in años.
2
I will be able to record a short introduction of my family, giving at least two ages with tener.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Small sounds, big difference

The family words hide three sound habits that instantly make you sound more natural. None of them is hard — they just work differently than in English. Today we lock them in, and then you use them on air.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you say family words out loud, what feels hardest so far?
📚 PART 2 · LA R SUAVE (a tap) 11 min

One quick tap of the tongue

A single r between vowels is not the English “r.” It's a light, single tap of the tongue against the ridge behind your top teeth — the same motion as the middle sound in the American English “water” or “ladder.” Say “pot of” fast and you'll feel it. Now use it in the family words:

  • el padreone tap: pa-dre
  • la madreone tap: ma-dre
  • el primoquick tap: pri-mo
  • el hermanosoft tap: her-ma-no
  • nuestro/aquick tap: nues-tro
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say this chain slowly five times, tapping each single r: padre – madre – primo – hermano. Then replay el padre and try to match the tap exactly — light and quick, never the heavy English r.
🧠 PART 3 · LA H MUDA (silent) 9 min

The letter you never say

In Spanish the h is always silent — you write it, but you never pronounce it. So hermano begins with the vowel sound “er-,” not a breathy English “h.” Listen and echo:

  • el hermanosay “er-MA-no” — no h
  • la hermana“er-MA-na”
  • el hijo“EE-ho” — no h
  • la hija“EE-ha”

You met tengo hambre yesterday. The h in hambre — do you say it? Decide, then reveal.

No — it's silent. Hambre sounds like “AM-bre.” The written h is there for spelling only. Same in hijo, hija, hermano.
🔈 PART 4 · LA Ñ EN AÑOS 5 min

The sound you need for ages

To give an age you say años — and the ñ makes the ny sound you already say in English “canyon” or “onion.” Careful: año (year) and ano (without the tilde) are not the same word — the tilde matters!

  • años (years) → A-nyos
  • niño (child) → NI-nyo
  • la señora (the lady) → se-NYO-ra

Practice the phrase you'll use most on today's recording: “Tengo quince años” — land that ny clearly in años.

🌎 CULTURA · LOS DOS APELLIDOS
Two last names, two families

In most Spanish-speaking countries, people carry two surnames: first the father's, then the mother's. So Rigoberta Menchú Tum carries Menchú (her father's family) and Tum (her mother's). It's a small, everyday reminder that a name holds both sides of a family — la familia de tu padre y la familia de tu madre.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Speaking a new language out loud — and recording it — takes courage. Everyone's first recordings feel awkward; that feeling is not a sign you're doing it wrong, it's a sign you're doing it at all. Rehearse, breathe, and press record. ¡Tú puedes!

🎤 PART 5 · TAREA ORAL 10 min

Your graded recording (15 points)

Record yourself introducing your family: say who is in it (at least three members, with a possessive like mi) and give at least two ages using tener. Aim for 25–45 seconds total. Every detail should be true for you.

📝 A MODEL (yours must be true for you)

«Hola. En mi familia somos cuatro. Tengo una hermana menor; ella tiene diez años. Mi madre tiene cuarenta años. Y yo tengo quince años.»
(Three members, a possessive, and two ages with tener.)

Need to hear the target verbs one more time before you record? Replay these models:

  • Yo tengo dos hermanos.tener — how many
  • Mi abuela tiene setenta años.tener — an age
  • Mi madre y mi padre son mis padres.possessives — mi / mis
