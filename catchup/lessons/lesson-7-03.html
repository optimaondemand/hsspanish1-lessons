Lesson 7.03
★ El sistema completo — Every Kind of Adjective
Lesson · about 40 minutes · the big grammar day
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This is the keystone lesson of Módulo 7 — the whole adjective system in one place.
Yesterday you learned the -o/-a four-form pattern. Today we finish the picture. Not every adjective takes four forms: some (like fuerte, valiente, grande) change only for number, never gender; and one small family (trabajador) adds an -a for the feminine. You'll also learn how to build plurals — including the accent surprise in joven → jóvenes — and where the adjective goes in the sentence (spoiler: usually after the noun). It's a denser day than usual, and that's on purpose: this is the lesson everything else in the module leans on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to sort adjectives into three families — four-form (-o/-a), gender-invariable (-e/consonant), and -or — and make each agree.
2
I will be able to form plurals (including joven → jóvenes) and place the adjective correctly — after the noun.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

One question before we start

Yesterday's rule: an -o adjective has four forms. Today's twist: not every adjective ends in -o. Some end in -e (fuerte, valiente) and some in a consonant (joven, trabajador). The question we answer today is: what do those do? Hold the four-form pattern in mind; we build out from it.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
A girl is brave. Yesterday's pattern said “change -o to -a.” But valiente ends in -e. What's your guess?
🧠 PART 2 · DOS FAMILIAS · -O/-A vs -E/CONSONANTE 11 min

Family 1: four forms  ·  Family 2: two forms

The ending of the adjective's masculine singular form tells you which family it belongs to.

Family 1 · ends in -O → four forms

Changes for gender & number.
alto · alta · altos · altas
Also: guapo, delgado, moreno, rubio, serio, gracioso, generoso, simpático.
alto · alta · altos · altas

Family 2 · ends in -E or consonant → two forms

Changes for number only — same for a boy or a girl.
fuerte · fuertes  /  joven · jóvenes
Also: valiente, paciente, inteligente, amable, grande.
él es fuerte · ella es fuerte

So Ella es alta (Family 1, feminine ending) but Ella es fuerte (Family 2, no separate feminine). There is no “fuerta” and no “valienta.”

🧠 PART 3 · LOS -OR Y LOS PLURALES 11 min

Family 3: the -or exception, and how to build plurals

A small group ends in -or (like trabajador, hard-working). These look like Family 2 — but they do add an -a for the feminine:

AdjectiveMasc. sing.Fem. sing.Masc. pl.Fem. pl.Listen
trabajadortrabajadortrabajadoratrabajadorestrabajadoras

Making plurals follows one simple split — and one word breaks a rule:

  • Ends in a vowel → add -s: alto → altos, fuerte → fuertes, grande → grandes.
  • Ends in a consonant → add -es: trabajador → trabajadores.
  • The wrinkle: joven → jóvenes. The plural adds -es and gains a written accent to keep the stress on the same syllable (JO-ven → JÓ-ve-nes).
Escuchar los plurales — altos · fuertes · trabajadores · jóvenes
🧠 PART 4 · LA COLOCACIÓN (PLACEMENT) 10 min

Where does the adjective go?

English puts the describing word before the noun: “a pretty house.” Spanish normally puts it after the noun: una casa bonita. Same idea, flipped order — and the adjective still agrees.

EnglishSpanish (noun + adjective)Listen
a pretty houseuna casa bonita
a tall boyun chico alto
She is a hard-working, funny girl.Ella es una chica trabajadora y graciosa.

(A handful of very common words — like bueno and grande — can sit before the noun in set phrases, and a few even shorten there. You'll meet those later; for now, the reliable rule is noun first, adjective after.)

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
Take three people you know and describe each with a full noun phrase, adjective after the noun and agreeing: una madre trabajadora, un amigo gracioso, unas primas valientes. Say each aloud and double-check the ending against its family — that's the whole system in action.

Son unas chicas pacientes. paciente ends in -e → Family 2 (no separate feminine), and the plural just adds -s → pacientes. Adjective comes after the noun.
