Lesson 8.02
★ Los cuatro sistemas — Ser, Estar, Tener, Gustar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Four verbs run everything you say about a person. Today they stand in one line so you can pick the right one on instinct.
You already know each verb on its own: ser (who someone is), estar (where they are / how they feel), tener (what they have — family, age, features), and gustar (what they like). The capstone skill is choosing among all four in the same breath — the way real speech does. You'll hear three sentences that use several systems at once, then practice picking the verb yourself. Still no new grammar — just the four you own, working together.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state the job of each verb — ser, estar, tener, gustar — and pick the right one for a given idea.
2
I will be able to understand a sentence that combines two or more of the systems at once.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

One idea, one verb

English uses "to be" and "to have" all over the place; Spanish is more precise. The trick is to ask what kind of idea you're expressing. Warm up by matching an idea to its verb.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of these do you find yourself mixing up the most?
🧠 PART 2 · LOS CUATRO SISTEMAS 10 min

The whole map on one page

VerbIts jobExample (yo)
seridentity, origin, nationality, lasting traitsSoy de México. Soy alto.
estarlocation + how you feel right nowEstoy en la escuela. Estoy contento.
tenerwhat you have: age, family, featuresTengo 15 años. Tengo dos hermanos.
gustarwhat you like (it is pleasing to you)Me gusta la música. Me gustan los deportes.

Two quick reminders: ser and tener can both describe looks — Soy moreno (I am dark-haired) OR Tengo el pelo moreno (I have dark hair). And gusta vs. gustan agrees with the thing liked, not with you.

🔊 PART 3 · TODO JUNTO 12 min

Hear the systems working together

Each sentence below stacks several systems in one breath. Click 🔊, listen twice, and try to name which verbs you hear. Read the English only after you've listened.

  • Soy… tengo… estoy…«Yo soy de México, tengo quince años y estoy contento.» — ser + tener + estar
  • Es… tiene… le gusta…«Mi hermana es alta, tiene el pelo largo y le gusta la música.» — ser + tener + gustar
  • Somos… estamos… nos gusta…«Nosotros somos estudiantes; estamos en la escuela y nos gusta aprender.» — ser + estar + gustar
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Replay each clip and shadow it — say it with the voice. Then swap in details that are true for you: your country, your age, your feeling, one thing you like. You've just drafted the opening of your Day-3 self-portrait.

Pick the right system

✅ TRY IT · choose the verb
«Mi hermano ___ dieciséis años.» (My brother ___ sixteen years old.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the verb
«A mí ___ los deportes.» (I like sports.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the verb
«Nosotros ___ en el parque ahora.» (We ___ in the park now.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the verb
«Ella ___ inteligente y simpática.» (She ___ smart and nice — lasting traits.)

Your turn: build one sentence that uses two systems, e.g. «I am tall and I have brown eyes.» Say it aloud, then reveal one good version.

Soy alto/a y tengo los ojos marrones. Soy (ser — a lasting trait) + tengo (tener — a feature). Two systems, one smooth sentence — exactly the capstone move.
🌎 CULTURA · UNA LENGUA, MUCHOS PAÍSES
El mismo verbo, veinte países

These four verbs sound the same whether you're in Mexico City, Bogotá, Buenos Aires, or Los Angeles — Spanish is the official language of some twenty countries and the second-most-spoken language in the world by native speakers. The words for feelings, family, and taste travel everywhere. When you say «me gusta», roughly half a billion people understand you instantly.

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Choosing the right verb, every time, is a small act of self-government: pausing to ask «what kind of idea is this?» instead of grabbing the first word. José de San Martín, the general who helped free Argentina, Chile, and Peru, then quietly stepped away from power, is the semester's figure for self-government — the discipline to do the harder, right thing. You'll meet him again in Lesson 8.05.
