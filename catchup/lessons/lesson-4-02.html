★ ¿Cuántos tienes? — The Verb tener
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In the last lesson you learned who is in a family. Today you learn the verb that lets you talk about them: tener (to have).
First you'll pick up a small set of words for ages and “my / your / his-her”. Then you'll meet tener, one of the most useful verbs in Spanish — it says how many brothers you have and, surprisingly, how old you are. We're learning tener's five forms on their own today; the special uses (ages, expressions, and how possessives agree) get the full treatment tomorrow. One new thing at a time.

🧠 PART 3 · EL VERBO TENER 12 min

Meet tener — “to have”

A verb changes its ending to match who is doing it — you saw this with -ar verbs and with estar. tener is a little special: the e in the stem changes to ie in most forms (tienes, tiene, tienen), but tenemos keeps the e. Click the 🔊 in each row to hear the example, then repeat it aloud.

SubjecttenerListen & repeat
yotengo Yo tengo dos hermanos. (I have two siblings.)
tútienes ¿Cuántos años tienes? (How old are you?)
usted / él / ellatiene Mi abuela tiene setenta años. (My grandma is 70.)
nosotros/astenemos Nosotros tenemos una familia grande. (We have a big family.)
ustedes / ellos / ellastienen Ellos tienen un primo en Guatemala. (They have a cousin in Guatemala.)

(In Latin America, ustedes covers “you all” in every setting. Vosotros, Spain's informal “you all,” you'll meet later, labeled as European. The yo form is the odd one out: tengo, not “teno” — worth memorizing on its own.)

✅ PART 4 · A PRACTICAR 11 min

Pick the right form of tener

All three sentences use tener — you're only choosing which form matches the subject. Glance back at the table above if you need it.

Your turn: how would you say “They have a cousin”? Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Ellos tienen un primo. “They” → tienen; the stem goes e → ie, just like tienes and tiene.
🌎 CULTURA · LA FAMILIA EXTENSA
Una casa, muchas generaciones

In much of Latin America — Guatemala included — family means more than parents and children. Grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins are often part of daily life, and it's common for los abuelos to live in the same home. That's why tener matters so much here: Tengo una familia grande is a sentence you'll hear with real pride.
