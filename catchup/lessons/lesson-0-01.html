★ Las cinco vocales — The Five Vowels
Lesson · about 40 minutes
👋 ¡BIENVENIDO/A A ESPAÑOL 1!
Welcome to Spanish — one of the most spoken languages on Earth, and now yours to learn.
Over the next few days (Módulo 0) you'll learn to read, pronounce, and greet in Spanish — the foundation everything else is built on. Here's the best news for an English speaker: Spanish is spelled the way it sounds. Once you own the sounds, you can read almost any word aloud correctly, even words you've never seen. And it all starts with just five vowels. Master these today and you're already speaking with a cleaner accent than most beginners ever reach.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pronounce the five Spanish vowels — a, e, i, o, u — each with one clean, short sound.
2
I will be able to explain why Spanish is easier to read aloud than English.

📚 PART 2 · LAS CINCO VOCALES 16 min

Five vowels, one clean sound each

English vowels slide around — the letter a sounds different in cat, cake, and father. Spanish is not like that. Each vowel has exactly one sound, and that sound is short and steady — it never glides. Learn these five and you've learned the heartbeat of Spanish pronunciation.

VowelSay it like…Keep it…
athe a in "father" (ah)short, open
ethe e in "bet" (eh)short, no glide to "ay"
ithe ee in "see" (ee)tight, brief
othe o in "for" (oh)round, no glide to "ow"
uthe oo in "food" (oo)short, lips forward

Listen, then echo each one

Click the 🔊 to hear each vowel spoken, then say it back out loud — short and clipped, no sliding. Say them in order five times: a – e – i – o – u.

  • a“ah” — open, like father
  • e“eh” — like bet
  • i“ee” — like see
  • o“oh” — like for
  • u“oo” — like food
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +6 min
There's a classic Spanish memory phrase for the five vowels: “a, e, i, o, u — el burro sabe más que tú” (a rhyming joke: "the donkey knows more than you!"). Say the five vowels aloud ten times, faster each round, keeping every sound short and pure. Record yourself on your phone and listen — can you hear any English glides sneaking in?

✅ TRY IT · no audio — use the rule
In Spanish, the vowel e sounds like the e in…

Your turn out loud: the word casa (house) has two of the same vowel. Say it, then reveal which vowel — and how it should sound both times.

Both are a — CA-sa — and in Spanish they sound identical: two short, open "ah" sounds. In English we'd weaken the second one; in Spanish you keep both crisp and equal. That steadiness is the whole secret.
👀 PART 4 · UN VISTAZO AL ALFABETO 4 min

A peek ahead: the alphabet

Those five vowels live inside the full Spanish alphabet, which has 27 letters — the same 26 as English, plus one extra letter all its own: the ñ (called eñe). You'll hear the ñ inside a real word in the next lesson. For now, just notice how steady these vowels stay when you hear them again.
