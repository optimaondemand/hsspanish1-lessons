★ ¿Cómo estás? — Feelings & the Verb estar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In the last lesson you learned where. Today you learn how you feel — and the verb that carries both.
First you'll pick up a set of feeling words (happy, tired, nervous, busy…). Then you'll meet estar, the verb of where you are and how you're doing right now. We're learning estar on its own today — its five forms, riding on the places and feelings you already know. The famous ser vs. estar question waits until tomorrow; one new thing at a time.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name how I feel in Spanish using common feeling words.
2
I will be able to use the five forms of estar (estoy, estás, está, estamos, están) with feelings and places.

Go through the list one more time, but this time picture a real person for each word — who in your life is ocupado/a? who was enojado/a yesterday? Attaching a word to a real face is one of the fastest ways to make vocabulary stick.
🧠 PART 3 · EL VERBO ESTAR 10 min

Meet estar — "to be" for where and how

A verb changes its ending to match who is doing it — you saw this with -ar verbs in Módulo 2. Here are the five forms of estar. Click the 🔊 in each row to hear the example, then repeat it aloud. Notice: every example is about a place or a feeling — that's what estar is for.

SubjectestarListen & repeat
yoestoy Yo estoy en casa. (I'm at home.)
túestás ¿Cómo estás? (How are you?)
usted / él / ellaestá Ella está cansada. (She's tired.)
nosotros/asestamos Nosotros estamos en la escuela. (We're at school.)
ustedes / ellos / ellasestán Ellos están ocupados. (They're busy.)

(In Latin America, ustedes covers "you all" in every setting — formal or casual. Vosotros, Spain's informal "you all," you'll meet later, labeled as European. The yo form is the odd one out: estoy, not "esto" — worth memorizing on its own.)

✅ PART 4 · A PRACTICAR 11 min

Pick the right form of estar

Both sentences use estar — you're only choosing which form matches the subject. Glance back at the table above if you need it.

✅ TRY IT · which form of estar?
"Yo ___ en la ciudad." (I ___ in the city.)
✅ TRY IT · which form of estar?
"Nosotros ___ contentos hoy." (We ___ happy today.)

Your turn: how would you say "She is nervous"? Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Ella está nerviosa. "She/he" → está; and the feeling word agrees — nerviosa for a girl, nervioso for a boy.
🌎 CULTURA · UNA PREGUNTA CON DOS RESPUESTAS
¿Cómo estás? vs. ¿Cómo está?

Across the Spanish-speaking world, greeting someone is a small act of courtesy. With a friend you'd ask ¿Cómo estás?; with an elder, a teacher, or a stranger you switch to the respectful ¿Cómo está? Same verb, different form — the choice quietly signals respect. A warm answer might be Estoy bien, gracias, ¿y usted?
